<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>What Does It Cost to Start an MVNO in 2026?</strong></h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The cost to start an MVNO depends on two choices: which MVNO model you pick, and whether you build your own network core or buy access through an MVNE. A<a href="https://spenza.com/mvno/mvno/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener"> mobile virtual network operator (MVNO)</a> sells mobile services under its own brand without owning towers or spectrum. It leases network access from a carrier and competes on price, niche, and customer experience.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">That single structural choice splits MVNO launch cost into three tiers. A branded reseller runs in the low tens of thousands. A light MVNO on an MVNE runs in the low hundreds of thousands in year one. A full MVNO that builds its own core runs into the millions. Each tier trades money and time for control.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Most brands do not need a full build. They start as a reseller or light MVNO, validate demand, then add control as scale justifies it. Launching as a full MVNO on day one is the most expensive way to learn that your pricing or target market needs work. To compare the models side by side, see<a href="https://spenza.com/mvno/mvno-types/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener"> MVNO Types Explained (2026)</a> and the broader<a href="https://spenza.com/mvno/mvno/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener"> MVNO hub</a>. The itemized table below shows exactly where the money goes.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Branded reseller: $10K to $50K, live in weeks</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">You sell mobile plans under your brand while your MVNE or host runs the stack. You control pricing, branding, and the customer relationship, and give up deep technical control. This is the fastest, cheapest way to start an MVNO and the right first step for most brands. See<a href="https://spenza.com/mvno/branded-reseller-mvno/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener"> Branded Reseller MVNOs</a> for real examples.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Light MVNO via an MVNE: $100K to $400K in year one</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">You take on more of the product, plan design, and customer data while the MVNE handles provisioning, billing, and carrier integration. You get more control over the experience without the cost of owning a core. Most brands that scale pick this path. Compare it in<a href="https://spenza.com/mvno/light-mvno-overview/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener"> Light MVNOs: An Overview</a>.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Full MVNO with its own core: $2M to $10M+, 12 to 18 months</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">You operate your own network core, IMSI range, and BSS, with direct carrier contracts. You get full control and the deepest differentiation, at the highest cost and longest timeline. This fits carriers, large scale players, and operators that need carrier-grade independence. See<a href="https://spenza.com/telecom/full-mvno/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener"> Decoding Full MVNOs</a> and the<a href="https://spenza.com/mvno/white-label-mvno-launch-guide-2025/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener"> White Label MVNO Launch Guide</a> for the build path.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>MVNO Wholesale Rates: What You Pay Carriers</strong></h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">MVNO wholesale rates are what you pay the host carrier for network access. You buy capacity per gigabyte or per line, then resell it at a margin. Rates are negotiated and differ by carrier, volume, and contract length, so there is no single public price. What you can plan around are the drivers.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>White-Label MVNO Pricing</strong></h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">White-label MVNO pricing is the lowest-cost on-ramp to a branded mobile service. Setup ranges from <strong>$10K to $400K</strong> depending on how much you customize, with gross margins between <strong>15 and 40 percent</strong> once you are live. A branded reseller model sits at the low end. A full white-label build with custom billing and deeper integration sits at the high end.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The cost model is OpEx, not CapEx. You pay a platform or per-line fee and the wholesale cost of the capacity you use, rather than building infrastructure. That is why a white-label launch can go live in about a week for a reseller model, or roughly 3 to 6 weeks for a deeper build. For the full path, see the<a href="https://spenza.com/mvno/white-label-mvno-launch-guide-2025/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener"> White Label MVNO Launch Guide</a>.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Build vs Buy: The Real Cost Decision Behind Starting an MVNO</strong></h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Strip away the tiers and the cost question becomes one decision: build your own MVNO, or buy access through an MVNE or white-label platform. Build means a full MVNO with millions in CapEx and a year or more of work. Buy means an MVNE or white-label model with OpEx that starts in the thousands and a launch measured in days to weeks.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>One-Time vs Ongoing MVNO Costs</strong></h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Budget MVNO launch cost in two buckets: one-time setup and recurring OpEx. Most founders underestimate the recurring side, which is where margin lives or dies.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>One-time costs</strong></h2>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Platform setup and integration</li>



<li>Branding, website, and launch marketing</li>



<li>Legal and FCC registration</li>



<li>Initial SIM or eSIM provisioning</li>
</ul>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Ongoing costs</strong></h3>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Wholesale data and voice from the carrier</li>



<li>Platform or per-line fees</li>



<li>Customer support and tooling</li>



<li>Marketing and customer acquisition</li>



<li>Compliance filings and USF contributions</li>
</ul>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Scale changes the math. A traditional full MVNO can run about $200K per month in OpEx at scale. A light MVNO on a per-line model pays far less because cost tracks usage, not a fixed buildout. That is the core reason the buy path protects cash flow.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>How Much Does an MVNO Make? The MVNO Business Model</strong></h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The MVNO business model is wholesale arbitrage. You buy network capacity at negotiated wholesale rates and sell retail plans at a margin, typically 30 to 50 percent gross margin on the connectivity itself. Profit then compounds through value-added services: device insurance, international packages, IoT data pools, premium support, and content bundles.</p>



<div style="background-color: #fff4e5; border-left: 5px solid #FF9800; padding: 1.4em 1.6em; margin: 2em 0; border-radius: 6px; font-family: Arial, sans-serif; box-shadow: 0 2px 5px rgba(0,0,0,0.05);">
<div style="color: #ff9800; font-size: 1.05em; font-weight: bold; margin-bottom: 0.9em;">Revenue Math Example</div>
<p style="margin: 0; line-height: 1.6;">Monthly revenue = <span style="font-weight: bold;">subscribers × ARPU</span>. Gross profit = <span style="font-weight: bold;">revenue × gross margin</span>. At <span style="font-weight: bold;">10,000 subscribers</span> on a <span style="font-weight: bold;">$30 plan</span>, that equals <span style="font-weight: bold;">$300,000 per month</span> in revenue and about <span style="font-weight: bold;">$120,000 per month</span> in gross profit at a <span style="font-weight: bold;">40 percent margin</span>, or roughly <span style="font-weight: bold;">$1.44M per year</span> before fixed costs. <span style="font-weight: bold;">Value-added services</span> can add to that.</p>
</div>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">How much an MVNO makes comes down to ARPU, margin, and churn. A subscriber who churns in month four never repays a $60 acquisition cost, so retention matters as much as growth. This is why niche MVNOs with built-in distribution, a device in the customer&#8217;s hand or an existing app, consistently outperform generic discount brands. For a full breakdown of revenue streams and cost centers, see<a href="https://spenza.com/mvno/mvno-revenue-models/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener"> How MVNOs Make Money</a>.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>How to Start an MVNO for Less</strong></h2>

<li><strong>Go eSIM-first. </strong>Skip SIM printing and shipping, and activate customers in minutes. See<a href="https://spenza.com/esim/esim-only-mvnos-2025/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener"> eSIM-Only MVNOs</a>.</li>



<li><strong>Pick a niche. </strong>A clear vertical (IoT, fintech, seniors, travel) lowers CAC and lifts retention.</li>



<li><strong>Avoid custom builds early. </strong>Custom billing and integrations are where budgets and timelines blow up.</li>



<li><strong>Bundle connectivity into an existing product. </strong>Adding mobile to a product you already sell turns connectivity into recurring revenue.</li>
