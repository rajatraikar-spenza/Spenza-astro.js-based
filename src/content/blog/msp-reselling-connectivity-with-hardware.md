<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>The &#8220;Connectivity Gap&#8221; in Your MSP Agreement</strong></h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Picture this:</strong> your enterprise client just ordered <strong>500 rugged tablets</strong> for their field operations team. You delivered the hardware on time, configured the devices, and deployed your management software. Everything’s ready to go.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Except it isn’t.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Now your client has to contact a <strong>carrier</strong> to procure 500 <strong>SIM cards</strong>, negotiate a <strong>data plan</strong>, manage provisioning, and figure out how to integrate connectivity into their device management workflow. What should have been a seamless deployment turns into a <strong>multi-vendor coordination nightmare</strong>.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Then, inevitably, a device goes <strong>offline in the field</strong>. Your client calls you. You point to the carrier. The carrier blames the hardware configuration. The client is stuck in the middle, frustrated, losing productivity, and questioning the value of your “<strong>managed</strong>” service.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">This is the <strong>connectivity gap</strong>, and it’s costing MSPs <strong>millions</strong> in lost revenue, strained client relationships, and growing competitive risk.</p>

<p style="margin-top: 0.5em; color: #333; line-height: 1.6em;">According to <a href="https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/managed-services-market" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"> industry research </a>, the global <strong>managed services market</strong> is projected to grow from <strong>$335 billion in 2024</strong> to over <strong>$731 billion by 2030</strong>. By bundling <strong>hardware and connectivity</strong> into a single, integrated, fully supported solution, they transform from simple device suppliers into indispensable strategic partners.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>The question isn&#8217;t whether to make this shift. It&#8217;s how quickly you can execute it.</strong></p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>The Shift: From Device Supplier to Solution Partner</strong></h2>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>The Old Model: Fragmented and Frustrating</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Let&#8217;s be honest about how most MSP-client relationships have traditionally worked:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>MSP Role:</strong> Hardware procurement, device provisioning, and break/fix support</li>



<li><strong>Carrier Role:</strong> Provides SIMs and a data plan through a separate agreement</li>



<li><strong>Enterprise&#8217;s Pain:</strong> Multiple vendors, multiple invoices, integration headaches, and zero single point of accountability</li>
</ul>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">When something goes wrong (and it always does), everyone points fingers. The MSP blames network issues. The carrier blames device configuration. IT directors waste hours troubleshooting what should be a simple connectivity problem.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">This fragmentation doesn&#8217;t just create operational inefficiency. It fundamentally undermines the value proposition of managed services.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>The New Model: Integrated and Indispensable</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Forward-thinking MSPs are rewriting the playbook entirely. Instead of stopping at hardware delivery, they&#8217;re providing a complete, ready-to-deploy solution:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>The device</strong> (via Hardware-as-a-Service)</li>



<li><strong>The optimized connectivity plan</strong> (multi-carrier, global coverage)</li>



<li><strong>Unified management and monitoring</strong> (single pane of glass)</li>



<li><strong>One point of accountability</strong> (true end-to-end support)</li>
</ul>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">This is the evolution from <strong>device supplier</strong> to <strong>solution partner,</strong> and it&#8217;s driving the industry&#8217;s most impressive growth metrics.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>The Challenge: How Can an MSP &#8220;Become&#8221; a Connectivity Provider?</strong></h2>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">This barrier has historically been significant. Let’s look at the traditional approaches and why they often fail:</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>The Old &#8220;Hard Way&#8221;: Becoming an MVNO</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Some MSPs have attempted to become full Mobile Virtual Network Operators (<a href="/mvno/mvno/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">MVNOs</a>), negotiating direct reseller agreements with carriers. This approach involves:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Multi-million dollar minimum commitments</li>



<li>Complex legal and regulatory compliance</li>



<li>Building or licensing connectivity management platforms</li>



<li>Hiring telecom-specific expertise</li>



<li>12-18 month implementation timelines</li>
</ul>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">For all but the largest MSPs, this path is prohibitively expensive, slow, and complex. It diverts resources from core competencies and introduces massive execution risk.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">If building an MVNO isn’t feasible, explore <a href="/mvno/mvno-alternatives/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">MVNO alternatives</a> for MSPs, resellers, and IoT providers.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>The Agent Model: The Low-Value Trap</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The simpler alternative has been to become a carrier agent, referring clients to major carriers in exchange for a small commission.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>The problems with this approach:</strong></p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Minimal margins (10-20%)</li>



<li>No customer ownership (carrier controls the relationship)</li>



<li>No brand differentiation (you&#8217;re just a referral channel)</li>



<li>Zero impact on business valuation</li>
</ul>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">This is connectivity reselling in name only. You aren’t building sustainable value; you’re simply collecting a referral fee.</p>
