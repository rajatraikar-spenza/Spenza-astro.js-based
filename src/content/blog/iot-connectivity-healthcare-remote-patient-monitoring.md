<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Challenges &amp; Barriers Still Slowing Progress</strong></h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Even as IoT connectivity in healthcare expands, challenges persist. The main hurdles come from three critical areas, reliability, security, and compliance.</p>



<h4 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>1. Network Downtime Risks</strong></h4>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">A one-minute downtime in a connected ICU can cause critical data loss. Basic networks fail when they face coverage gaps or single-carrier dependency. Experts estimate that traditional IoT networks cause intermittent data loss in medical transmissions, risking incomplete records.</p>



<h4 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>2. Security Concerns</strong></h4>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Healthcare data breaches surged past 88 million compromised records. Each connected device increases risk. Secure healthcare IoT must encrypt every byte, protect endpoints, and isolate sensitive medical traffic.</p>



<h4 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>3. Compliance Pressure</strong></h4>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Every connected medical device must meet HIPAA and GDPR standards. With 10–15 devices per hospital bed now connected, compliance oversight becomes complex. Noncompliance can lead to fines or data exposure, eroding patient trust.</p>



<h4 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>4. Integration Issues</strong></h4>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Many IoT systems don’t integrate smoothly with <strong>Electronic Health Records (EHRs).</strong> Data silos slow care. Compliant medical device connectivity platforms must bridge these systems seamlessly.</p>



<h4 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>5. Cost &amp; Complexity</strong></h4>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Implementing multi-network IoT in healthcare often involves multiple vendors, carrier contracts, and monitoring tools. Simplified, unified platforms like Spenza remove these obstacles through global eSIMs and single dashboards, cutting cost and confusion.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">These issues reveal why standard IoT connectivity simply isn’t enough for medical systems. Healthcare needs a clinical-grade backbone built for life-critical reliability and absolute trust.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Once healthcare overcomes these hurdles, the next step is expansion, scaling connectivity through AI, automation, and predictive intelligence.</p>
