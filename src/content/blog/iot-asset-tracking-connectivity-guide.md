<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>What Connectivity Does an Asset Tracker Need?</strong></h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">At its core, an IoT asset tracker needs three things:</p>



<ol class="wp-block-list">
<li>A way to determine location (typically GNSS technologies such as GPS).</li>



<li>A method for sending that data, such as <a href="https://spenza.com/esim/nb-iot-vs-lte-m-vs-5g-redcap/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">LTE-M</a>, NB-IoT, Cat-1bis, RedCap, satellite connectivity, or a hybrid of these options.</li>



<li>A platform to manage connectivity, monitor devices, and control lifecycle operations.</li>
</ol>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The challenge is that there is no universal answer to the question of which connectivity technology is best.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">A shipping container, scooter, and video-enabled fleet system all have different connectivity requirements.Many organizations choose SIM providers before defining their connectivity requirements. Instead, the network should be selected based on the use case. The network should be selected based on the use case.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">That’s why this guide takes a requirements-driven, practical framework approach rather than pushing a single network or SIM strategy.</p>



<div style="background-color: #fff4e5; border-left: 5px solid #FF9800; padding: 1.4em 1.6em; margin: 2em 0; border-radius: 6px; font-family: Arial, sans-serif; box-shadow: 0 2px 5px rgba(0,0,0,0.05);">
<div style="color: #ff9800; font-size: 1.05em; font-weight: bold; margin-bottom: 0.9em;">Did You Know?</div>
<p style="margin: 0; line-height: 1.6;">Most <span style="font-weight: bold;">failed asset-tracking deployments</span> are not caused by <span style="font-weight: bold;">GPS hardware</span>. They are more often the result of <span style="font-weight: bold;">coverage gaps</span>, <span style="font-weight: bold;">roaming restrictions</span>, <span style="font-weight: bold;">battery drain</span>, or <span style="font-weight: bold;">network selection decisions</span> made too early in the project without considering long-term deployment conditions.</p>
</div>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>GNSS: How Trackers Know Where They Are</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Connectivity and location are often discussed together, but they perform different functions within an asset-tracking solution.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">GNSS (Global Navigation Satellite System) technologies such as GPS, Galileo, GLONASS, and BeiDou determine the location of an asset. Cellular or satellite networks then transmit that location data back to applications, operators, and fleet management platforms.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Most modern trackers combine GNSS positioning with cellular connectivity. The GNSS module calculates where the asset is, while LTE-M, NB-IoT, Cat-1bis, RedCap, or satellite connectivity ensures that information reaches the cloud.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">For most deployments, the question is not whether to use GNSS, it is how to combine location technology with the right connectivity architecture to balance coverage, battery life, and cost.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>LTE-M</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">LTE-M has emerged as one of the most popular connectivity technologies for mobile asset tracking.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">It was purpose-built for IoT use cases that need low power operation while still supporting mobility. Unlike certain LPWAN technologies, LTE-M is capable of maintaining reliable connectivity for moving devices, making it well-suited for trucks, trailers, fleet vehicles, and mobile cargo applications.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Another advantage is support for firmware-over-the-air updates. For most mobile tracking applications, LTE-M represents the best balance between battery life, coverage, and performance.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>NB-IoT</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">NB-IoT focuses on power efficiency above all else.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">It performs exceptionally well for devices that send small amounts of data infrequently and remain relatively stationary.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">NB-IoT is well suited for smart parking, utility infrastructure, and other stationary assets due to its low power consumption and strong coverage.&nbsp;</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">However, mobility support can be inconsistent depending on operator implementation.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">For that reason, NB-IoT is often less attractive for assets that regularly cross regions or countries.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Cat-1bis</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Cat-1bis is increasingly becoming the default option for general-purpose cellular IoT deployments.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Many organizations choose Cat-1bis because it offers broad carrier support and avoids some of the regional availability challenges associated with LTE-M.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">While power consumption is somewhat higher than <a href="https://spenza.com/esim/nb-iot-vs-lte-m-comparison/">NB-IoT or LTE-M</a>, the technology supports higher throughput and works well for connected vehicles, industrial equipment, and mobile assets requiring frequent communication.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>5G RedCap</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><a href="https://spenza.com/telecom/what-is-5g-redcap-iot-iiot-guide-2025/">5G Reduced Capability</a>, commonly known as RedCap, fills an important gap between traditional IoT connectivity and full-scale broadband.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The technology is particularly relevant for applications involving:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Driver monitoring</li>



<li>Dashcams</li>



<li>Video analytics</li>
</ul>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Traditional trackers transmit coordinates and sensor data.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Modern fleet platforms increasingly transmit images, diagnostics, and video.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">These applications require significantly more bandwidth than LTE-M or NB-IoT can efficiently provide.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">RedCap is designed specifically for this next generation of connected assets.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Satellite and NTN Connectivity</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">No cellular network covers every location.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Shipping lanes, remote mines, offshore energy infrastructure, and wilderness environments frequently operate beyond the reach of terrestrial networks.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">This is where satellite connectivity becomes valuable.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Many modern deployments use a hybrid architecture that combines cellular and satellite connectivity.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">When cellular coverage is available, devices use LTE-M or Cat-1bis.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">When coverage disappears, trackers automatically switch to satellite communications.</p>



<div style="background-color: #fff4e5; border-left: 5px solid #FF9800; padding: 1.4em 1.6em; margin: 2em 0; border-radius: 6px; font-family: Arial, sans-serif; box-shadow: 0 2px 5px rgba(0,0,0,0.05);">
<div style="color: #ff9800; font-size: 1.05em; font-weight: bold; margin-bottom: 0.9em;">Expert Insight</div>
<p style="margin: 0; line-height: 1.6;">The key question is not whether <span style="font-weight: bold;">satellite connectivity</span> is better than <span style="font-weight: bold;">cellular connectivity</span>. The real decision is whether the business impact of <span style="font-weight: bold;">losing asset visibility</span> outweighs the additional cost of maintaining satellite coverage. For many remote, mission-critical, or cross-border deployments, the cost of downtime can far exceed the premium paid for resilient connectivity.</p>
</div>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The ideal connectivity solution varies based on how an asset operates, the amount of data it generates, its location, and its power requirements.</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Container tracking</strong> often requires a combination of cellular and satellite connectivity to maintain visibility across international routes, ports, and remote locations.</li>



<li><strong>Fleet and vehicle tracking</strong> typically depend on LTE-M or Cat-1bis to support continuous movement, reliable handovers between networks, and regular data transmission.</li>



<li><strong>High-value asset monitoring</strong> benefits from resilient connectivity strategies, including multi-network access and failover mechanisms, to minimize the risk of losing critical tracking information.</li>



<li><strong>Micro-mobility and smart infrastructure applications</strong> such as scooters, bicycles, and parking systems are well suited for NB-IoT due to their low data requirements and focus on extending battery life.</li>



<li><strong>Cold-chain logistics</strong> require dependable connectivity to consistently transmit both location and environmental conditions such as temperature or humidity, making LTE-M a common choice.</li>



<li><strong>Video-enabled tracking and driver safety systems</strong> demand higher bandwidth to support cameras, AI processing, and advanced analytics, making technologies like RedCap more suitable.</li>
</ul>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Ultimately, the most effective connectivity option is not necessarily the lowest-cost one—it is the solution that best aligns with the operational, coverage, and performance needs of the deployment.</p>

<p style="margin: 0; line-height: 1.6;">The <span style="font-weight: bold;">best network</span> is not necessarily the <span style="font-weight: bold;">cheapest network</span>. It is the one that best aligns with the <span style="font-weight: bold;">operational requirements</span> of the asset being tracked, including coverage, reliability, latency, power consumption, mobility, and total cost of ownership over the device&#8217;s lifecycle.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Multi-Network Failover and SIM Steering</strong></h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">One of the most overlooked causes of tracking failures is network dependence.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Many organizations assume that broad coverage claims automatically translate into reliable performance.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">In practice, coverage and performance are not the same thing.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">A device may technically have access to multiple networks in a region but remain attached to a single preferred carrier due to roaming policies or SIM steering configurations.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">This creates hidden reliability risks.</p>



<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter size-large is-resized"><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" width="1024" height="576" src="/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Multinetwork-connectivity-failover-1024x576.png" alt="Multi-network failover for IoT asset tracking

<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>What Is SIM Steering?</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">SIM steering is the practice of directing devices toward preferred partner networks.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">From the connectivity provider&#8217;s perspective, this may reduce wholesale costs.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">From the customer&#8217;s perspective, it can create problems.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">A steered SIM may continue attempting to connect to the preferred network rather than switching immediately to the better option.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The consequences can include:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Delayed transmissions</li>



<li>Packet loss</li>



<li>Failed registrations</li>



<li>Increased battery consumption</li>



<li>Reduced tracking accuracy</li>
</ul>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">These issues can be difficult to diagnose.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>FPLMN Lock-In and Hidden Connectivity Issues</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Some roaming implementations use <strong>FPLMN (Forbidden Public Land Mobile Network)</strong> lists to prevent devices from reconnecting to networks that previously failed registration attempts. While intended to improve network behavior, these restrictions can sometimes prevent trackers from attaching to stronger available networks.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">For moving assets, this can create a frustrating scenario. A stronger network may be available, but the device continues attempting to reconnect to a previously preferred carrier. The result can be delayed reporting, packet loss, higher latency, and unnecessary battery drain.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">For fleets operating across multiple regions, these hidden restrictions can quietly reduce tracking reliability without triggering obvious alerts.</p>



<div style="background-color: #fff4e5; border-left: 5px solid #FF9800; padding: 1.4em 1.6em; margin: 2em 0; border-radius: 6px; font-family: Arial, sans-serif; box-shadow: 0 2px 5px rgba(0,0,0,0.05);">
<div style="color: #ff9800; font-size: 1.05em; font-weight: bold; margin-bottom: 0.9em;">Myth vs Reality</div>
<p style="margin: 0; line-height: 1.8;"><span style="font-weight: bold;">Myth:</span> If a SIM operates across <span style="font-weight: bold;">200 countries</span>, it effectively eliminates coverage issues. <br /><br /><span style="font-weight: bold;">Reality:</span> Coverage availability does not guarantee <span style="font-weight: bold;">network quality</span>. The ability to connect to the <span style="font-weight: bold;">best available network</span> often matters more than the total number of countries listed on a coverage map, especially for mission-critical or high-availability deployments.</p>
</div>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Why Multi-Network Failover Matters</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Moving assets rarely remain inside a single network footprint.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">A fleet vehicle may travel through multiple carrier territories in a single day. A shipping container may cross dozens of countries over its lifetime.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Multi-network architectures improve resilience by allowing devices to switch between carriers when conditions change.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Modern implementations typically use:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Multi-IMSI technology</li>



<li>eUICC-enabled eSIMs</li>



<li>Dynamic profile switching</li>



<li>Multi-carrier agreements</li>
</ul>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The result is greater uptime, improved performance, and reduced operational risk. For asset-tracking deployments, failover should be viewed as a business continuity feature rather than a connectivity enhancement.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Global Coverage, Roaming, and Regulation</strong></h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Global asset tracking introduces challenges that go beyond coverage.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Historically, many IoT deployments relied on <a href="https://spenza.com/esim/what-is-iot-roaming/">permanent roaming</a> arrangements to provide international connectivity.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">While effective in some situations, regulators in several countries have increased scrutiny of long-term roaming models.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Countries such as Brazil and Turkey have implemented restrictions that affect how foreign SIMs can operate over extended periods.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">For organizations deploying trackers globally, this creates a new requirement: connectivity must be compliant as well as functional.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>The Shift Toward Local Profiles</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">This is one reason eSIM technology has become increasingly important.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Rather than relying on a single roaming profile indefinitely, eSIM-enabled devices can download and activate local operator profiles when required.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">This approach offers several advantages:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Better compliance</li>



<li>Improved coverage</li>



<li>Lower roaming costs</li>



<li>Reduced operational risk</li>
</ul>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Leading global IoT connectivity providers may offer access to <strong>500–680+ operator networks worldwide</strong>. While broad coverage is important, organizations should also evaluate failover capabilities, local profile support, and network quality across deployment regions.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">For device manufacturers building global products, local-profile capability is rapidly becoming a strategic requirement rather than a nice-to-have feature.</p>



<div style="background-color: #fff4e5; border-left: 5px solid #FF9800; padding: 1.4em 1.6em; margin: 2em 0; border-radius: 6px; font-family: Arial, sans-serif; box-shadow: 0 2px 5px rgba(0,0,0,0.05);">
<div style="color: #ff9800; font-size: 1.05em; font-weight: bold; margin-bottom: 0.9em;">Expert Insight</div>
<p style="margin: 0; line-height: 1.6;">Global connectivity is no longer just about reaching <span style="font-weight: bold;">more countries</span>. Long-term success depends on maintaining <span style="font-weight: bold;">regulatory compliance</span>, <span style="font-weight: bold;">network performance</span>, and <span style="font-weight: bold;">operational flexibility</span> across every market where devices are deployed.</p>
</div>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Battery Life vs Connectivity</strong></h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Battery life is one of the most important design constraints in asset tracking.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">A tracker that requires frequent battery replacement can quickly become expensive to maintain, especially when devices are deployed across large fleets or remote locations.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The good news is that modern connectivity technologies provide multiple tools for optimizing power consumption.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The challenge is understanding the trade-offs.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>How Network Choice Affects Battery Life</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">NB-IoT generally offers the longest battery life because it is optimized for infrequent, low-volume transmissions.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">LTE-M delivers strong efficiency while maintaining support for mobile assets.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Cat-1bis and RedCap consume more power but provide additional bandwidth and flexibility.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The right choice depends on the use case.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">A parking sensor transmitting once per day has different requirements than a vehicle tracker transmitting every five minutes.</p>



<div style="background-color: #fff4e5; border-left: 5px solid #FF9800; padding: 1.4em 1.6em; margin: 2em 0; border-radius: 6px; font-family: Arial, sans-serif; box-shadow: 0 2px 5px rgba(0,0,0,0.05);">
<div style="color: #ff9800; font-size: 1.05em; font-weight: bold; margin-bottom: 0.9em;">Power-Saving Technologies</div>
<p style="margin: 0; line-height: 1.6;"><span style="font-weight: bold;">PSM (Power Saving Mode)</span> allows a device to remain asleep for extended periods while staying registered with the network, minimizing energy consumption without requiring a full network reattachment. <br /><br /><span style="font-weight: bold;">eDRX (Extended Discontinuous Reception)</span> reduces how frequently the device wakes up to listen for incoming network messages, further extending battery life while maintaining periodic reachability. <br /><br />Together, these technologies enable <span style="font-weight: bold;">LTE-M</span> and <span style="font-weight: bold;">NB-IoT</span> trackers to achieve <span style="font-weight: bold;">battery lifespans exceeding 10 years</span> in some low-power, low-data deployments.</p>
</div>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Why Signal Quality Matters</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Battery consumption is not determined solely by the network technology.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Signal quality plays a major role.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">When coverage is weak, devices must transmit at higher power levels and may attempt repeated network registrations.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">This increases energy consumption dramatically.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Poor network selection strategies, including aggressive steering, can therefore reduce battery life even when the underlying hardware is optimized.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Reporting Frequency Trade-Offs</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Reporting frequency often has a larger impact on battery life than network selection.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Consider two identical trackers:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Tracker A reports every 5 minutes</li>



<li>Tracker B reports every 4 hours</li>
</ul>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Tracker B may operate for years longer on the same battery.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Organizations should balance visibility requirements against power constraints.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">More data is not always better data.</p>



<div style="background-color: #fff4e5; border-left: 5px solid #FF9800; padding: 1.4em 1.6em; margin: 2em 0; border-radius: 6px; font-family: Arial, sans-serif; box-shadow: 0 2px 5px rgba(0,0,0,0.05);">
<div style="color: #ff9800; font-size: 1.05em; font-weight: bold; margin-bottom: 0.9em;">Key Takeaway</div>
<p style="margin: 0; line-height: 1.6;">The <span style="font-weight: bold;">longest battery life</span> comes from optimizing the entire system, not just choosing a larger battery. <span style="font-weight: bold;">Network technology</span>, <span style="font-weight: bold;">signal quality</span>, <span style="font-weight: bold;">reporting intervals</span>, and power-saving features such as <span style="font-weight: bold;">PSM</span> and <span style="font-weight: bold;">eDRX</span> all work together to maximize device lifespan.</p>
</div>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>What Tracker Connectivity Costs</strong></h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Connectivity pricing is often less transparent than hardware pricing.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Most providers advertise monthly SIM costs while giving limited visibility into the broader economics of operating a tracking fleet.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The reality is that connectivity costs extend beyond the SIM itself.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Major Cost Drivers</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Several factors influence the total cost of IoT connectivity:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Data usage:</strong> Low-data GPS trackers cost significantly less than video-enabled applications that transmit large volumes of data.</li>



<li><strong>Coverage requirements:</strong> Global deployments often involve higher costs due to roaming and multi-region connectivity needs.</li>



<li><strong>Redundancy:</strong> Multi-network connectivity improves reliability but may increase overall connectivity expenses.</li>



<li><strong>Lifecycle management:</strong> Provisioning, monitoring, diagnostics, and support contribute to the total cost of ownership.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Building and Scaling Asset Tracking Deployments</strong></h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">As asset-tracking deployments grow, manual connectivity management becomes impractical. Scaling from hundreds to thousands of devices requires automation, centralized visibility, and lifecycle management.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Connectivity decisions should start during device design, as module selection impacts network compatibility, power efficiency, certification, and future scalability.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">For device manufacturers, eSIM provisioning enables flexible global deployments and simplifies manufacturing and compliance. For fleet operators, a centralized platform is essential for managing activations, usage, troubleshooting, and device operations at scale.</p>

As fleets grow, the ability to provision, monitor, troubleshoot, and optimize <span style="font-weight: bold;">thousands of connected devices</span> from a <span style="font-weight: bold;">single management platform</span> often becomes a significant operational and competitive advantage.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Conclusion</strong></h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The most important lesson in asset tracking connectivity is simple: choose the network based on the use case, not the other way around.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">LTE-M, NB-IoT, Cat-1bis, RedCap, and satellite connectivity all have legitimate roles within modern tracking deployments. The right choice depends on how assets move, how much data they transmit, how long batteries must last, and where those assets operate.</p>
