<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Telecom Regulations: The Basics and Beyond</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Every country sets its own telecom rules. In the U.S., MVNOs must comply with:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>FCC regulations</strong>: These rules define how MVNOs access carrier networks and protect consumers. You must follow FCC processes to get licensed and approved, especially for interconnection and porting.</li>



<li><strong>CALEA compliance</strong>: Under this federal mandate, MVNOs must allow government authorities to perform lawful intercepts. Your network setup must include the technical ability to route call data to law enforcement when authorized.</li>



<li><strong>E911 requirements</strong>: Emergency calls must connect immediately and send location data. This applies to every mobile user on your network, whether they use SIM or eSIM. Non-compliance can result in severe penalties.</li>



<li><strong>STIR/SHAKEN</strong>: These protocols prevent caller ID spoofing. If you allow voice calling, your system must verify the caller’s identity digitally. This cuts down robocalls and helps build trust with users.</li>
</ul>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Other countries have their own versions. Missing even one requirement can delay your launch.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Privacy &amp; Data Security Compliance</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Data privacy is no longer optional. It&#8217;s enforced through strict rules like:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>GDPR</strong> in Europe</li>



<li><strong>CCPA</strong> in California</li>



<li><strong>SoC2 compliance telecom</strong> requirements for operational security</li>
</ul>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">MVNOs must build trust with users. That starts with proving data is secure. You can’t just say it is, you must show it through audits and certifications.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Identity &amp; Financial Compliance</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Know Your Customer (KYC) isn’t just for banks anymore. Many telecom regulators now demand identity checks before activating SIMs or eSIMs. You also need the right to sell services, which means:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Reseller licenses</li>



<li>Network agreements</li>



<li>Number provisioning rights</li>
</ul>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">All of these vary by market.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Telecom Tax Compliance&nbsp;</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Taxation for MVNOs is extremely complicated. You&#8217;re not just collecting sales tax. You must apply:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Telecom-specific fees</strong>: These charges apply only to communication services and vary by type of offering, voice, text, or data. Each carries different rate rules per region.</li>



<li><strong>Local 911 surcharges</strong>: These are location-based fees collected to fund emergency response infrastructure. They differ from state to state and sometimes by county.</li>



<li><strong>Universal service funds (USF)</strong>: A federal-level fee meant to ensure basic telecom access across all areas, including rural ones. MVNOs must contribute based on reported revenues.</li>



<li><strong>City/state-specific tariffs</strong>: These include taxes and regulatory fees imposed by state and local governments. They are layered over federal obligations and require highly accurate calculation and collection.</li>
</ul>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">That changes not only by country, but by region and sometimes even zip code.</p>
