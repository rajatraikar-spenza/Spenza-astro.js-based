<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Kids Smartwatch IoT Connectivity Case Study</strong></h3>



<blockquote class="wp-block-quote is-layout-flow wp-block-quote-is-layout-flow">
<p class="wp-block-paragraph">This kids smartwatch connectivity case study shows how Spenza enabled a wearable brand to launch connected devices across the US, UK, and Europe with data, voice, SMS, automated SIM activation, Shopify checkout integration, and automated reseller and enterprise billing. The network configuration was completed in two months, and the first production devices shipped within three months of contract signing.</p>
</blockquote>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>The Situation</strong></h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">A kids smartwatch brand focused on child safety and GPS tracking came to us with a clear goal: ship a product that works the moment a parent takes it out of the box. No hunting for SIM cards. No carrier signup forms. No activation delays.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">They wanted data, voice, and SMS services live from the first power-on, across the US, UK, and Europe. And they needed it all to plug into their existing Shopify storefront so customers could buy the watch and a connectivity plan in a single checkout.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Simple to describe. Hard to pull off.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>What Made This Difficult</strong></h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><a href="/iot/iot-connectivity-comparison/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">IoT connectivity</a> for consumer wearables is not the same as connecting a fleet of sensors or utility meters. The smartwatch needed to behave like a phone: real voice calls, text messages that show up on a parent’s regular phone (publicly routed SMS, not app-only messaging), and enough data for GPS tracking and firmware updates.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Most large carriers don’t offer publicly routed SMS on IoT SIM plans. It’s not a standard configuration. So the brand had already burned weeks talking to operators who couldn’t support their use case.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">On top of the connectivity challenge, they had commercial problems too:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>One-step activation at checkout. A parent buys the watch on <a href="/spenza-product/sell-devices-esims-shopify-spenza/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">Shopify</a>, picks a plan, and the SIM should activate automatically before the package even ships. No manual provisioning on Spenza’s side or the brand’s side.</li>



<li>Reseller and enterprise billing. The brand sells through multiple channels. Each reseller needs accurate markup calculations, commission tracking, and invoicing. They were doing all of this manually in spreadsheets.</li>



<li>Global coverage from day one. The watch had to work across three regions without requiring different SKUs or separate carrier contracts for each market.</li>
</ul>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>How Spenza Solved the Smartwatch Connectivity Challenge</strong></h2>



<div class="wp-block-group is-layout-constrained wp-block-group-is-layout-constrained">
<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The solution required more than a standard IoT SIM. The smartwatch needed publicly routed SMS, voice, data, global coverage, automatic activation, and an e-commerce flow that connected the hardware purchase with the connectivity subscription.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Spenza addressed these requirements through three connected capabilities:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Custom network configuration:</strong> A network profile supporting publicly routed SMS, voice, and data, with APN configuration optimized for the smartwatch.</li>



<li><strong>Automated connectivity activation:</strong> Shopify checkout was connected to the Spenza platform so the selected SIM and plan could be activated automatically when the order was confirmed.</li>



<li><strong>Automated commercial operations:</strong> Parent-child billing handled reseller pricing, commissions, enterprise accounts, and invoicing without manual spreadsheet reconciliation.</li>
</ul>
</div>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>What We Built</strong></h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Spenza </strong>used its connectivity aggregation platform, powered by <strong><a href="https://www.aeris.com/resources/spenza-delivers-seamless-global-connectivity-for-a-kids-smartwatch-brand/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">Aeris</a></strong>, to deliver a multi-service SIM card with global reach. The project had three pieces:</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>1. Custom Network Configuration</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Over a two-month collaborative sprint with Aeris, we stood up a network profile that supported publicly routed SMS alongside voice and data. The APN configuration was tuned specifically for the smartwatch’s operating system, so connectivity worked reliably without draining the device’s small battery.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">For context, non-standard network setups with large operators typically take four to six months. We had this ready in two.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>2. Shopify Checkout Integration</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">We connected our platform directly to the brand’s Shopify store. When a customer adds a smartwatch to their cart, they see connectivity plans as an upsell. If they pick one, the SIM activates at the moment the order is confirmed. By the time the fulfillment team picks the watch off the shelf, it’s already live on the network.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">No API calls for the brand to manage. No manual provisioning steps. The integration handles plan selection, activation, and payment processing in one flow.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>3. Automated Parent-Child Billing</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The brand sells through its own site, through resellers, and through enterprise accounts (schools, for example). Each channel has different pricing, markups, and commission structures. Before Spenza, someone on their team spent hours each month reconciling all of this in spreadsheets.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">We set up automated parent-child billing that handles the math across every channel. Reseller invoices, enterprise billing, and commission calculations all run without manual intervention.</p>
