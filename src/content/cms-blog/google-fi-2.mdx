<p>Google Fi is a U.S. mobile service from Google that runs on T-Mobile&#8217;s network. It sells four plans: Flexible at $20 for one line plus $10 per GB, and Unlimited Essentials, Standard and Premium at $35, $50 and $65 for one line. Google says every plan includes nationwide 5G on a 5G-capable phone.</p>
<p>This guide covers what Fi is, what each plan includes, whether Fi has 5G and what that means in practice, which phones work, and how it behaves when you travel. It is a consumer service, but the way it is built, a brand and a plan structure on top of someone else&#8217;s towers, is the same model that businesses use to launch their own.</p>
<p><em>Last verified 5 November 2026 against <a href="https://fi.google.com/about/plans/" rel="noopener">fi.google.com/about/plans</a>. Plans, prices and promotions change often, so confirm them on Google&#8217;s site before you switch.</em></p>
<div class="mh-callout"><p><strong>Short answer:</strong> Yes, Google Fi has 5G on all four plans. It is delivered through the T-Mobile network, and there is no 5G surcharge. The plan you pick decides your data allowance, hotspot, and whether data works abroad, not whether you get 5G.</p></div>
<h2 id="what-is-google-fi">What Google Fi is and how it works</h2>
<p>Google Fi Wireless is a mobile virtual network operator, or MVNO. That means Google does not build its own cell towers. It buys wholesale access to an existing network and sells service under its own brand, plans and app. Fi&#8217;s coverage page says it provides nationwide 4G LTE and 5G coverage through the T-Mobile network. Our guide to <a href="/mvno/mvno/">what an MVNO is</a> covers the model in more detail, and the <a href="/mvno/mno-mvno-mvne-mvna-roles/">roles of MNO, MVNO, MVNE and MVNA</a> explain who owns which layer.</p>
<p>What Fi adds on top is a set of services Google builds itself. On phones sold on fi.google.com, Fi can connect you to more than one carrier network and move you to the one its analysis says will give the best experience where you are. It routes calls over Wi-Fi or cellular, whichever works better. Every plan also includes a VPN by Google, advanced spam call blocking, and Google Account security.</p>
<p>You sign up in the Fi app, activate by eSIM from home, and transfer your number there too. Google says number transfers usually take about 20 minutes. There is no annual service contract, and no activation or cancellation fee.</p>
<h2 id="plans-and-pricing">Google Fi plans and pricing</h2>
<p>Fi sells two kinds of plan: a flexible plan where you pay for the data you use, and three unlimited plans that differ in allowance and extras. Prices below are monthly, before taxes and government fees, which Google says are normally between 10 and 20 percent.</p>

<h3 id="flexible">Flexible: pay for the data you use</h3>
<p>Flexible charges a monthly fee for unlimited calls and texts, $20 for the first person and $15 for each additional one, and then $10 for every GB of data at home and abroad. It suits someone who lives mostly on Wi-Fi. It also includes hotspot tethering and data-only SIMs for tablets and laptops. Google says a bill-protection feature caps what you pay, so a heavy month does not run away from you.</p>
<h3 id="unlimited">The three unlimited plans</h3>
<p>Unlimited Essentials is the cheapest unlimited option and is built for use in the U.S., with no hotspot. Unlimited Standard adds 25 GB of high-speed hotspot data and coverage for data, calls and texts in Canada and Mexico. Unlimited Premium adds 50 GB of high-speed hotspot data, international data in 200+ destinations, free calls to 50+ destinations, 6 months of YouTube Premium and 100 GB of Google One storage.</p>
<p>All three keep working after you pass the allowance, but at 256 kbps until the next cycle, which is only enough for light tasks such as messaging. If you want faster data back before the cycle ends, Fi sells it for $10 per GB. Select smartwatches get full connectivity on every plan.</p>

<h2 id="google-fi-5g">Does Google Fi have 5G?</h2>
<p>Yes. Google&#8217;s FAQ says that if you have an Android phone or iPhone that supports 5G, you get nationwide 5G on all Fi plans. There is no separate 5G plan and no add-on. On Unlimited Premium and Flexible, Fi also offers 5G abroad in many of the destinations it covers, on 5G-compatible devices.</p>
<p>Fi lists three 5G capabilities. <strong>mmWave 5G</strong> gives the fastest speeds but is available in select urban areas only. <strong>Standalone 5G</strong> uses 5G technology end to end, so it can reach places where 4G is not available. <strong>5G DSDS</strong>, Fi&#8217;s dual connect technology on Pixel phones, lets the phone hold two networks at once, including a 5G one, so it can reach the better signal faster. The 3GPP, the standards body behind 5G, describes the <a href="https://www.3gpp.org/technologies/5g-system-overview" rel="noopener">5G system architecture</a> that standalone mode is based on.</p>
<h3 id="5g-reality">What 5G on Fi means in practice</h3>
<p>5G access is a property of three things together: the network, your location and your phone. Fi supplies the network access and the plan. It does not control whether a tower near you carries mmWave or a mid-band layer, or whether your handset supports every band. A phone that can only use older bands will connect, but at LTE-level speeds.</p>
<p>Earlier versions of this article quoted coverage percentages for Fi, such as 36 percent 5G and 43 percent 4G, and compared them with other prepaid brands. We could not trace those figures to a current, named source, so we removed them. Google points to its own coverage map instead, and independent maps and tests give a better read on a specific address. Check the map for the places you actually use the phone, such as home, the office and your commute.</p>
<h3 id="speed-and-congestion">Speed, congestion and data priority</h3>
<p>Older reviews said Fi customers sit behind T-Mobile&#8217;s own subscribers when a tower is busy. Google&#8217;s current coverage page says otherwise: Fi is not subject to data traffic deprioritization, so you should not see slower speeds just because the network is crowded. We cannot test that claim independently here, so treat it as Google&#8217;s statement. Two limits do exist and are documented. After your plan&#8217;s high-speed allowance, data slows to 256 kbps. And on all plans video may stream at standard definition (480p).</p>

<h2 id="coverage-and-switching">Coverage and network switching</h2>
<p>Fi&#8217;s main footprint is T-Mobile&#8217;s. For phones bought from fi.google.com, Fi can also connect to other carrier networks, in the U.S. and abroad, and it decides when to switch based on its analysis of what gives the best experience at your location. Google says it adjusts in real time if the current network is weak, and keeps learning from factors such as new towers and new radio frequencies.</p>
<p>A bring-your-own phone may not get all of this. Google lists Pixel, Samsung Galaxy and Motorola phones in its shop, and iPhones work on Fi, but the multi-network behavior is described for phones sold on fi.google.com. Rural users in particular should check the coverage map for their exact locations before moving a main line, because a service built on one primary network can leave gaps where another carrier is stronger.</p>
<p>On select Pixel phones, Fi also offers Wi-Fi Auto Connect+, which places the phone on high-quality Wi-Fi in some crowded venues such as airports and malls, with encrypted traffic. Data used there does not count toward your monthly allowance.</p>
<h2 id="phones-and-esim">Phones, eSIM and bringing your own</h2>
<p>You can buy a phone from Fi or bring your own. Fi sells Google Pixel, Samsung and Motorola devices, and Pixel and other phones sold there get the best integration. For a phone you own, the main requirements are that it is unlocked and supports the T-Mobile network, which Google says for data-only SIM devices means a GSM radio. A phone still locked to its original carrier will not move over. Our <a href="/byod/mvno-locked-phone-challenge-guide/">guide to locked phones on MVNOs</a> explains why that happens.</p>
<p>Fi advertises eSIM activation from home through the app. If you are new to the technology, our <a href="/esim/esim/">eSIM explainer</a>, the guide to <a href="/telecom/mobile-esim-plans/">mobile eSIM plans</a> and the piece on <a href="/esim/dual-sim-esim/">dual SIM and eSIM</a> cover how profiles are installed and what to check on your handset.</p>
<p>Beyond phones, Unlimited Premium and Flexible include data-only SIMs for compatible tablets and laptops. The devices must be unlocked and support T-Mobile, and the data counts against your monthly high-speed allowance. Select Pixel Watch and Samsung Galaxy Watch models get full connectivity at no extra charge on every plan.</p>
<h2 id="travel">Using Google Fi when you travel</h2>
<p>Travel is where Fi&#8217;s plans differ most. Unlimited Premium and Flexible include data in 200+ destinations without extra setup or a day pass. Unlimited Standard covers data, calls and texts in Canada and Mexico only, and Unlimited Essentials is meant for use in the U.S. Fi&#8217;s pages quote two different counts for international 5G destinations, so we do not repeat either.</p>
<p>Check three details before you fly. First, international data on Premium has its own high-speed allowance and then slows to 256 kbps. Second, calls abroad are charged per minute, and Premium adds free calls from the U.S. to 50+ destinations. Third, Fi publishes a rate table by country at <a href="https://fi.google.com/about/international-rates/" rel="noopener">fi.google.com/about/international-rates</a>, and that is the place to confirm a specific destination.</p>
<h2 id="pros-cons">Who Google Fi suits, and who should look elsewhere</h2>
<p>Fi is a good match for people who value clear pricing, regular international travel, and Google&#8217;s account and security features. The flexible plan helps light data users, and the unlimited plans give a simple family structure with up to six people. Group plans need everyone to use the same plan type, so it is not a way to mix a cheap line with a premium one.</p>
<ul><li><strong>Strengths:</strong> no annual contract or activation fee, 5G on every plan, international data on Premium and Flexible, included VPN and spam blocking, smartwatch connectivity, and pausing a line for up to three months.</li><li><strong>Limits:</strong> one primary host network, so rural performance depends on T-Mobile&#8217;s footprint; a 256 kbps ceiling after the allowance; hotspot unavailable on Essentials; and promotions that change, so the headline price may differ from what a new line pays.</li></ul>
<p>Reviews are mixed in the usual way. People like the billing and the travel experience. They complain about rural coverage and, on some devices, about the Fi app using background data. We could not verify a specific figure for the app&#8217;s data use, so we do not quote one. If it matters, watch the usage screen in your first week.</p>
<h2 id="business-view">What Fi tells a business about the MVNO model</h2>
<p>Companies that give staff connectivity can learn from how Fi works. The brand, the plans, the app and the customer service are Fi&#8217;s. The network is a partner&#8217;s. For an employer, the same split appears in <a href="/telecom/connectivity-as-a-benefit/">connectivity as an employee benefit</a>, where a company chooses which parts to run itself.</p>
<p>Fi is a consumer product, though. Businesses that want their own branded service, with their own plans, billing and SIM or eSIM management, need an MVNO platform rather than a retail plan. The guides to <a href="/mvno/mvno-types/">MVNO types</a>, <a href="/mvno/white-label-mvno/">white-label MVNOs</a> and <a href="/mvno/mvno-launch-cost/">what it costs to launch an MVNO</a> are the right starting points. Spenza provides a platform for that use case, but nothing about Fi requires it, and for a household or an individual a retail plan like Fi&#8217;s is usually all you need.</p>
<h2 id="conclusion">Conclusion</h2>
<p>Google Fi is a straightforward MVNO on T-Mobile with four plans, 5G included on all of them, and international data included on Premium and Flexible. Pick Flexible if you use little data, Essentials if you want the cheapest unlimited line in the U.S., Standard if you need hotspot and Canada and Mexico, and Premium if you travel widely or want the extras. Before you switch, check the coverage map at your own address, confirm your phone is unlocked and compatible, and read the plan page on the day you sign up, because prices and offers move.</p>
