<p>If you operate a voice platform, AI agent or contact center, &#8220;the call succeeded&#8221; leaves several questions unanswered. A signaling session can succeed while voicemail answers. A receiving endpoint can accept a call while audio works in only one direction. A handset can ring while displaying a warning that discourages the recipient from answering.</p>
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="section-1" style="font-weight:700;"><strong>Separate six layers of evidence</strong></h2>
<p>Record what you observed at each layer. A combined score needs the supporting observations so your team can distinguish an identity problem from a media or routing failure.</p>

<p>With A-attestation, the originating provider asserts that it knows the customer and has verified their authorization to use the calling number. That claim belongs to the authentication row. <a href="https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8588.html">RFC 8588</a> specifies the claim used to express the originating provider&#8217;s attestation. It does not certify delivery or the recipient’s experience.</p>

<p>Display deserves its own row even when audio is excellent. A business-name lookup and the actual incoming screen are different observations. Likewise, a warning does not establish that audio delivery failed. Keep both results so the team responsible for each issue can act on evidence.</p>
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="section-2" style="font-weight:700;"><strong>Read the SIP trace without overclaiming</strong></h2>
<p>Session Initiation Protocol (SIP) is used to set up and control communication sessions. A SIP trace records signaling at the points where it was captured. You still need endpoint observations to establish what the recipient experienced.</p>

<p>The supported conclusion is that signaling progressed and voicemail accepted the session. Human pickup, upstream intelligibility and consumer-screen appearance remain unobserved.</p>
<p>Ringback is especially easy to misread. A caller can hear a locally generated tone or early media. <a href="https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc3960.html">RFC 3960</a> explains early media and ringing-tone generation; the sound in the originating call is not direct evidence of an audible alert on a particular receiving phone.</p>
<p>After a 200 response to INVITE, determine what answered. Voicemail, an interactive voice response (IVR) menu and a controlled test responder can all accept a session without the intended person participating. Then test media separately. Accepted signaling does not prove intelligible two-way speech.</p>
<p>Apply the same discipline to failures. A rejection or timeout needs route context and supporting observations. Do not turn a SIP status into a spam-label diagnosis without evidence of that classification.</p>
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="section-3" style="font-weight:700;"><strong>Build a reproducible call-delivery test</strong></h2>
<p>Use controlled endpoints and agreed test traffic. Before placing calls, define the behavior your service requires and the evidence you will collect.</p>
<p>For a controlled conversation test, an example acceptance rule is: the intended endpoint alerts, the expected responder is identified, an agreed phrase is audible in each direction, and speech continues after a transfer if transfer is in scope. Choose timing thresholds from your service requirements before the run. Report pass when the required evidence meets those criteria, fail when you observe a mismatch, and inconclusive when required evidence is missing. Mark optional layers you did not test as unobserved.</p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" style="font-weight:700;"><strong>1. State the question and the tested route</strong></h3>
<p>Write a narrow objective, such as whether the agent&#8217;s production route reaches a designated endpoint with audible speech in both directions. Record the originating number, provider, route configuration and application version. Note any forwarding, transfer or fallback behavior relevant to the scenario.</p>
<p>Run the test through that path. A manual call from an employee&#8217;s mobile phone cannot validate an AI agent&#8217;s SIP trunk or media connection. A call leg is one connection segment, such as the agent-to-recipient leg or a new connection created during a transfer. If the agent hands a caller to a human, identify the transfer leg as a separate part of the test.</p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" style="font-weight:700;"><strong>2. Define the receiving environment</strong></h3>
<p>Record the destination network, endpoint type, device model or SIP software, and relevant app settings. A SIP responder and a consumer handset answer different questions. If handset behavior matters, include a handset observation in the plan.</p>
<p>For display testing, record whether the originating number is saved as a contact. Also capture call filtering, Do Not Disturb, forwarding and voicemail settings. A familiar contact name or local filter can change the observed experience, so keep these conditions consistent or identify them as separate scenarios.</p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" style="font-weight:700;"><strong>3. Correlate the evidence before interpreting it</strong></h3>
<p>Use a shared time reference and preserve the time zone. Attach originating call IDs, destination events and any separate leg identifiers to one test case. Record attempt time, signaling progress, answer time, media onset (when audio begins) and termination where available.</p>
<p>Mark the source of each field. An application webhook, carrier trace and handset screen capture may describe different parts of the call. Do not merge their timestamps into one timing metric unless you know what the interval measures.</p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" style="font-weight:700;"><strong>4. Capture authentication as its own result</strong></h3>
<p>Record the attestation and verification information exposed by your provider. Store the source field as well as the interpreted value. Missing information should be &#8220;unavailable,&#8221; not silently converted into authentication failure.</p>
<p><a href="https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/trusted-calling-with-shakenstir">Twilio&#8217;s trusted-calling documentation</a> explains the difference between attestation and validation. Other interfaces may expose different representations, so check the provider&#8217;s definition rather than assuming similarly named fields are interchangeable.</p>
<p>For Twilio, keep three interfaces distinct: incoming webhook <code>StirVerstat</code> reports available verification information; outbound status-callback <code>StirStatus</code> reports assigned attestation; the Calls API exposes <code>stir_verstat</code> through the <a href="https://www.twilio.com/en-us/changelog/voice-api-call-resource-returns-stir-shaken-status">Call resource update</a>. Save the field name, interface, direction and raw value. An originating A assertion is not itself a receiving verification result.</p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" style="font-weight:700;"><strong>5. Observe alerting and identify the answering endpoint</strong></h3>
<p>On a controlled handset, observe whether an incoming alert appears and whether the device audibly rings under its current settings. A SIP probe is a controlled test endpoint. Record the events it can observe and identify them as probe results.</p>
<p>Identify what answered. A short scripted response from a test responder can identify that endpoint; voicemail should remain voicemail. An automated answer rate must not be presented as a human pickup rate.</p>
<p>If the call never appears on the receiving device, preserve that negative observation alongside the originating trace. It narrows the investigation without claiming which intermediate system caused the failure.</p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" style="font-weight:700;"><strong>6. Test audio in both directions</strong></h3>
<p>Have the agent and receiving endpoint each speak an agreed phrase. Verify what the other side hears. Check whether the beginning is clipped, whether there is unexpected silence, and whether speech survives pauses and interruptions. For an AI agent, include the opening prompt and one realistic turn change.</p>
<p>Where available, collect codec, packet loss, jitter and media timing. Identify which connection segment and direction each measurement covers. A healthy metric from one leg does not describe the entire path. Label an estimated mean opinion score as an estimate, and retain the underlying measurements where possible.</p>
<p>Record upstream and downstream separately. For the agent-to-destination leg, upstream means the agent&#8217;s phrase as heard at the destination; downstream means the destination&#8217;s phrase as heard at the agent. Save the phrase, observer, timestamp and result for each direction. Attach packet loss or jitter to its measured leg and direction; a provider-side metric alone does not prove end-to-end intelligibility.</p>
<p>For a transfer scenario, repeat the audio check after the handoff. Working audio before transfer cannot establish that the new leg works.</p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" style="font-weight:700;"><strong>7. Capture display and warnings independently</strong></h3>
<p>Record the exact incoming name, number and warning on the chosen interface. Preserve a screenshot or identify the source that supplied the observation. Include its timestamp and configuration, and keep sensitive recipient details out of shared reports.</p>
<p>If the test has no handset or supported label-observation source, mark display as unobserved. Neither an authenticated caller ID nor successful probe arrival fills that gap. Verizon&#8217;s <a href="https://spamalerts.verizon.com/vsf/">call feedback guidance</a> explicitly treats authentication as only one factor in call treatment.</p>
<p>For a reproduced warning or incorrect name, continue with <a href="/telecom/ai-agent-calls-spam-likely-carrier-guide/">AI-agent spam-label troubleshooting by carrier</a>, preserving the exact display and receiving settings.</p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" style="font-weight:700;"><strong>8. Retest with a comparison and controlled changes</strong></h3>
<p>Keep a baseline record before changing configuration. Repeat the same scenario after a fix, using comparable endpoints and settings. When practical, change one variable at a time so the result is interpretable.</p>
<p>Include the number of attempts and the denominator for every reported rate. Define exclusions before drawing conclusions. If only some calls have media or display evidence, show those sample sizes separately. A small controlled test can diagnose an issue without establishing a population-wide delivery rate.</p>
<p>The <a href="/telecom/voip-phone-number-warm-up-testing/">VoIP number warm-up and testing guide</a> provides related number-lifecycle context. Keep that work connected to repeatable observations rather than assuming time or traffic alone has resolved the issue.</p>
