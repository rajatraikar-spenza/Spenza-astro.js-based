The request usually lands mid-week. A system integrator has just won a contract for 600 refrigerated trailers. It needs SIMs, a shared data pool and a monthly invoice with its own logo on it, all within ten days. If your MVNO or IoT connectivity business can't turn that around, the integrator signs with whoever can. Channel sales let a connectivity provider turn one wholesale agreement into hundreds of branded accounts, but only when plans, margins and provisioning are built for resellers from day one.

## Pick partners who already own the customer

The best channel partners already sit inside the customer's technology budget. Marketing reach matters far less. Take a Central Valley agricultural business that relies on [Bakersfield managed IT services from Diamond IT](https://www.diamondit.pro/). That managed IT firm already holds the routers, endpoints and support contract. Adding a cellular line for a failover router or a remote soil sensor is a plan change on an existing account. Nobody has to onboard a new vendor.

Device makers and system integrators work the same way. A tracker manufacturer shipping 20,000 units a year can embed your SIM at the factory, and every unit it sells becomes a recurring line. Before signing anyone, write down three facts for each partner type: the device it sells, the typical monthly data per device, and who answers the phone when a unit goes offline.

That last question decides more partnerships than price does.

## Size the opportunity before setting prices

Partners will ask how big the market is, and the numbers help you. According to [Ericsson](https://www.ericsson.com/en/reports-and-papers/mobility-report/dataforecasts/iot-connections-outlook), total cellular IoT connections reached around 4.5 billion at the end of 2025 and are expected to approach 8 billion by the end of 2031.

| Metric | Figure |
| --- | --- |
| Total cellular IoT connections, latest count | About 4.5 billion worldwide |
| Forecast cellular IoT connections, six years out | About 7.8 billion, roughly 10% compound annual growth |
| Broadband and Critical IoT (4G/5G) connections | About 2.6 billion, almost 60% of cellular IoT |
| Total IoT connections, all technologies | 22.3 billion now, forecast 47.1 billion, about 13% CAGR |
| Legacy network shutdowns completed | 80 providers fully off 3G, 46 fully off 2G |

Show partners the shutdown row first. Alarm panels, elevator phones and older fleet trackers still running on 3G need new hardware and a new plan. An installer with thousands of those units in the field already has its next sales campaign.

## Build plans with margin tiers baked in

Start with your wholesale cost and stack three prices on top of it: what you charge the partner, the partner's suggested retail price, and what the end customer actually pays. Take a 10 MB pooled IoT line. If wholesale costs you $0.90 per SIM per month, list the partner price at $1.40 and recommend retail at $2.25. You keep about 36% gross margin. The partner keeps about 38%, enough to justify the time its sales team spends on the line.

Then tier partners by volume instead of negotiating every deal. A simple ladder works: list price under 1,000 active SIMs, 8% off between 1,000 and 10,000, and 15% off above that. Set overage rates and pooling rules per tier, so one heavy month from a partner's biggest customer can't quietly wipe out your margin. Tools for [controlhub plans and billing pricing](https://spenza.com/controlhub/) let you clone a wholesale plan into partner versions and track margin per SIM as usage shifts. You see a tier going underwater before the invoice runs, not after.

## Wire up provisioning and reseller billing

A partner judges you by how fast its first SIM goes live. Write the workflow down before launch, and make every step something the partner can trigger without emailing your team.
- Create the partner account with its own branding, plan catalog and price tier attached.
- Allocate a SIM or eSIM inventory batch to the partner, tagged by ICCID range so ownership is never ambiguous.
- Let the partner assign SIMs to end customers and activate them through an API call or portal button.
- Sync usage daily and send automatic alerts when a customer pool passes 80% of its allowance.
- Generate two invoices from the same usage record: yours to the partner and the partner's to its customer.

Most reseller programs break at that two-invoice step. If the partner rebuilds its customer bill in a spreadsheet from a CSV you export, disputes start within the first quarter. When one usage record feeds both invoices, there is nothing to argue about.

## Plan for eSIM and multi-network coverage

Partners selling into logistics, agriculture and utilities rarely know which network will be strongest where a device ends up. A trailer that leaves a depot in Fresno can spend the week crossing three states and several coverage footprints. Offer multi-network SIMs as a standard plan option rather than a custom request, and price the premium clearly so partners can pass it straight through to their customers.

eSIM changes the inventory conversation as well. With remote SIM provisioning, a device maker can ship hardware with a starter profile and switch to the production profile once the unit reaches its customer. The partner no longer has to guess months ahead which plan a pallet of devices will need. The GSMA's SGP.32 specification was written for IoT devices with no screen or user interface, so ask your platform team where it sits on the roadmap.

Whatever you offer, write the coverage terms into the partner agreement. State which networks each plan can attach to, how roaming is billed, and what happens when a device sits in a country the plan doesn't cover. Partners can live with a high price they understand. A surprise roaming charge on their customer's invoice is what ends the relationship.

## Give partners a support model they can run

A white-label partner puts its own name on your service, so when a SIM stops working, the end customer calls the partner first. Define the support split in writing. The partner handles first-line issues such as activation questions, lost devices and plan changes. You handle network faults, provisioning failures and anything that touches the core platform. Publish response times for each, for example four business hours for a single dead SIM and one hour for an outage that affects a whole pool.

Then give the partner the data it needs to close first-line tickets on its own: last network attach, signal quality, data session history and the time of the last successful connection. Most "my device is offline" calls turn out to be a power problem, a used-up data pool or a device stuck on old firmware. A technician who can see the session history solves those in minutes instead of escalating them to you.

Track four numbers per partner every month: active SIMs, average data per SIM, tickets per 100 SIMs and cancelled lines. A partner with rising tickets and flat activations needs training. A partner with rising activations and frequent overage needs a bigger pool, which is also an easy upsell for you.

## Help managed IT providers bundle and scale

Managed service providers sell flat monthly fees per seat or per site, so connectivity has to fit that model. Hand them a ready-made bundle. A "site continuity" package might include one failover router SIM on a 5 GB pool and two sensor SIMs for temperature or door monitoring, priced as a single per-site line. An MSP covering 40 client offices can add that to every renewal without rewriting its contracts.

Give the MSP's help desk the controls too. From a branded portal, technicians should be able to suspend a lost SIM, swap a router's line or check last-seen data without opening a ticket with you. Every ticket you avoid is a margin you keep.

For the first 90 days, review each new partner's activities every two weeks. A partner that signs but activates fewer than ten SIMs in its first month usually needs a sales one-pager, not a discount.

So start small. Choose one partner type, launch three plans for it, and hold yourself to one number: every newly signed partner gets its first SIM active within 48 hours. If you can do that reliably, the integrator with 600 trailers and a ten-day deadline signs with you.
