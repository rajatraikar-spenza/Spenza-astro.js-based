<p>Skype is gone. Microsoft retired it on May 5, 2025, after more than two decades, and pointed users to Microsoft Teams Free. So a Skype vs Zoom vs Teams comparison now means something different: which of the platforms that outlived Skype fits your work, and what covers the phone-number half of what Skype used to do. Short answer: use Teams if you live in Microsoft 365, Google Meet if you live in Google Workspace, and Zoom if you mostly meet people outside your company. Cheap calls to real phone numbers need a calling plan, not a meeting app.</p>
<p><em>Last verified 5 November 2026.</em> Dates come from Microsoft&#8217;s Skype retirement page, and plan limits from each vendor&#8217;s own pages. Prices and limits change often, so confirm them before you buy.</p>
<h2 id="what-happened">What happened to Skype</h2>
<p>Microsoft&#8217;s own notice is short: as of May 5, 2025, Skype is retired, and Skype for Business is not affected. On that date, calls, SMS, call forwarding, voicemail and Caller ID setup stopped working. Users could sign in to Teams Free with their Skype credentials, and their chats and contacts carried over automatically. Chats with Teams work or school accounts, Skype for Business history, and Copilot or bot content did not transfer. The full notice is on <a href="https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/skype/skype-is-retiring-in-may-2025-what-you-need-to-know">Microsoft Support</a>.</p>
<p>The reason is not mysterious. Microsoft already sold Teams for chat, meetings and calling inside Microsoft 365, and running two overlapping products split its effort. Microsoft has not published a single official list of reasons, so treat any detailed explanation of the decision as interpretation.</p>
<h2 id="timeline">Skype retirement timeline</h2>
<p>Several dates matter, and a few have already passed. If you are only checking now, the export and number-porting windows are closed.</p>
<div class="mh-table"><table><thead><tr><th>Date</th><th>What Microsoft says happened</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>April 3, 2025</td><td>Calling and Skype Number subscriptions stopped auto-renewing. <p>Microsoft&#8217;s page is not perfectly consistent. One FAQ entry mentions January 2026 for data availability, while other sections give April 1 and June 15, 2026. Where the sources disagree, this guide uses the dated entries above and tells you to confirm with Microsoft rather than guess.</p>
<h2 id="skype-for-business">Skype for Business: what is still supported</h2>
<p>People mix up consumer Skype and Skype for Business, which caused real planning errors. They have different lifecycles.</p>
<div class="mh-table"><table><thead><tr><th>Product</th><th>Position</th><th>Source</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Consumer Skype</td><td>Retired May 5, 2025</td><td>Microsoft Support retirement page</td></tr><tr><td>Skype for Business Online</td><td>Retired July 31, 2021</td><td>Microsoft Lifecycle</td></tr><tr><td>Skype for Business Server 2015 and 2019</td><td>End of support October 14, 2025: no more security updates, bug fixes or technical support. <p>A server that keeps running after October 14, 2025 is not safe by default. It gets no security patches, so any new flaw stays open. Microsoft also ran a paid Extended Security Update program as a bridge, and said its second period would end in October 2026 with no further extensions. Microsoft&#8217;s recommendation is to migrate to Teams or upgrade to Subscription Edition. See the <a href="https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/lifecycle/products/skype-for-business-server-2019">Skype for Business Server 2019 lifecycle page</a> for the details.</p>

<h2 id="comparison">Zoom vs Teams vs Google Meet at a glance</h2>
<p>Skype is in the first column only for reference. These are the limits each vendor publishes for its free and paid plans, as checked in November 2026.</p>
<div class="mh-table"><table><thead><tr><th></th><th>Skype</th><th>Zoom</th><th>Microsoft Teams</th><th>Google Meet</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Status</td><td>Retired May 5, 2025</td><td>Active</td><td>Active</td><td>Active</td></tr><tr><td>Free group meeting length</td><td>n/a</td><td>40 minutes</td><td>60 minutes</td><td>60 minutes</td></tr><tr><td>Free participants</td><td>n/a</td><td>100</td><td>100</td><td>100</td></tr><tr><td>Paid meeting length</td><td>n/a</td><td>Up to 30 hours (Pro)</td><td>Up to 30 hours</td><td>Up to 24 hours</td></tr><tr><td>Paid participants</td><td>n/a</td><td>100 (Pro), 300 (Business), 1,000 (Enterprise)</td><td>300 on Teams Essentials and Microsoft 365 consumer plans</td><td>Up to 1,000</td></tr></tbody></table></div>
<p>Sources: <a href="https://zoom.us/en/pricing">Zoom pricing</a>, Microsoft&#8217;s Teams Free pages, and Google&#8217;s Meet plan information. Larger Teams business plans publish their own limits.</p>
<p>Free-tier length is the only place where the three differ meaningfully for casual use. Teams and Meet give you 60 minutes per group meeting, Zoom 40. Everything else on a free plan is the same 100-person cap, so the decision rests on integration and guest experience rather than on a feature checklist.</p>
<h2 id="what-each-is-for">What each platform is for</h2>
<h3>Microsoft Teams</h3><p>Teams is the platform Microsoft moved Skype users onto. It combines chat, meetings, file sharing and calling in one app, and it sits next to Word, Excel and Outlook. If your company already pays for Microsoft 365, Teams is probably already in the subscription, which makes it the default for internal collaboration. Teams Free covers everyday calls and chat. Telephone calling to ordinary numbers is a separate Teams Phone license plus PSTN connectivity, which is a different buying decision from a meeting.</p>
<h3>Zoom</h3><p>Zoom is a standalone meeting product, which makes it the neutral choice when your participants are spread across different companies and different suites. Because it does not depend on Microsoft or Google accounts, it is the common pick for sales demos, interviews and webinars. The free plan allows 100 participants for up to 40 minutes. Zoom Pro is listed at $14.16 per user per month billed annually, and Business at $18.33, which lifts the cap to 300 participants. Zoom Phone is sold separately for calling.</p>
<h3>Google Meet</h3><p>Google Meet is built into Google Workspace. Anyone with a Google Account can host up to 100 participants for up to 60 minutes on the free plan, and one-to-one calls have no time limit. Paid Workspace plans allow group calls of up to 24 hours and up to 1,000 participants. If your team already lives in Gmail and Calendar, Meet needs almost no setup.</p>
<p>Pricing is where comparisons mislead. Teams and Meet are usually bought as part of a productivity suite that also includes email and documents, while Zoom is bought as a meeting product. Compare the full bill for what you actually need, not the sticker price of the meeting component. Each vendor also sells AI meeting summaries on different terms that change often, so check the current terms on each pricing page rather than relying on a table from months ago.</p>
<h2 id="calling">Replacing Skype&#8217;s phone calling</h2>
<p>The half of Skype most comparisons skip is cheap calling to ordinary phone numbers. Zoom, Teams and Meet are meeting tools. Dialing a landline in another country needs a calling plan with PSTN access, such as Teams Phone, Zoom Phone or Google Voice, or a separate calling service. Price that separately and test it with the numbers you really call.</p>
<p>Check three things before you commit: who owns the number and the account, how inbound calls route when someone is out, and what happens when the call has to reach a human. A working demo does not prove that a customer dialing your published number reaches the right person. If you are weighing number types for verification messages, our guide to <a href="/telecom/non-voip-vs-voip-numbers/">non-VoIP versus VoIP numbers</a> explains why the answer differs by service.</p>
<p>If you held a Skype Number and did not port it, the window has closed. Microsoft says subscriptions ended in May 2026 and port-out requests are no longer accepted. The practical steps now are to publish a new number, update your website, directory listings, invoices and automated messages, and tell customers. Do not promise recovery of the old number.</p>
<h2 id="network">Moving to Teams is a network project</h2>
<p>Organizations coming from Skype for Business Server often treat Teams as a software swap. It is also a network change. The old system ran on your own servers and mostly on your own network. Teams runs in Microsoft&#8217;s cloud, so every call crosses your internet connection.</p>
<p>Microsoft&#8217;s <a href="https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoftteams/prepare-network">network preparation guide</a> gives per-endpoint bandwidth figures. Audio needs about 58 kbps in each direction at the recommended level. Group video meetings are recommended at roughly 2.5 Mbps down and 4 Mbps up per endpoint, and up to 4 Mbps each way for best performance. Screen sharing in meetings is recommended at 2.5 Mbps. Teams adapts, and Microsoft says it can deliver HD video in under 1.5 Mbps, but a branch office with fifty people on camera adds those numbers up quickly.</p>
<p>The same page recommends several fixes worth planning before cutover:</p><ul><li><strong>Split-tunnel VPN.</strong> Send Teams traffic directly to Microsoft rather than through the corporate VPN. Microsoft notes that VPNs are not designed for real-time media, may not support UDP, and can add latency by hair-pinning traffic through the VPN device.</li><li><strong>Quality of Service.</strong> Prioritize voice and video packets on managed network segments, so unexpected events do not degrade calls.</li><li><strong>Wi-Fi.</strong> Plan access point placement and use the 5 GHz band where possible, since consumer-grade wireless is often not tuned for real-time media.</li><li><strong>Monitoring.</strong> Use the Call Quality Dashboard to find the sites and users that have problems.</li></ul>
<figure><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" src="https://media.spenza.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/skype-vpn-hairpin-vs-direct-orange.webp" alt="Two laptops send video traffic to a cloud: the top ribbon loops through an office server labelled VPN loop, while the bottom ribbon runs straight to the cloud, labelled Direct path." width="1600" height="900" loading="lazy"><figcaption>Hair-pinning Teams traffic through a VPN adds a long loop; a split tunnel sends it straight to the cloud.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Remote workers are the weak point. Once Teams traffic goes directly to the internet, call quality depends on each person&#8217;s home broadband and Wi-Fi, which you do not manage. Some organizations give key staff a second path, for example a business eSIM line on a laptop or phone, so a failing home connection does not drop a customer call. Our guides to <a href="/esim/business-internet-failover/">business internet failover with eSIM</a> and <a href="/esim/esim-for-enterprises-business-enabler-2025/">eSIM for enterprises</a> explain how that works. It is an option to test, not a guarantee.</p>
<h2 id="checklist">A continuity checklist for the move</h2>
<p>Close the project against recorded results, not against licenses purchased or apps installed. A short list that holds up:</p><ol><li><strong>Inventory what depends on Skype.</strong> List users, numbers, SIP trunks, desk phones, integrations and any server. Separate retired accounts from active carrier services.</li><li><strong>Split the jobs.</strong> Meetings and chat go to a meeting platform. Public calling goes to a phone service with an owner for each number.</li><li><strong>Pick the platform by existing stack.</strong> Microsoft 365 points to Teams, Google Workspace to Meet, and mixed external audiences often to Zoom. Many teams run two.</li><li><strong>Pilot real tasks.</strong> Include remote staff, the branch with the weakest link, and an inbound customer call that has to transfer to a person.</li><li><strong>Fix the network.</strong> Apply split tunneling, QoS and Wi-Fi changes, then measure again under a normal busy day.</li><li><strong>Decommission carefully.</strong> Retire unsupported Skype servers only after retention needs are met and customer contact details are updated.</li></ol>
<p>Connectivity costs are part of the same decision. Mobile plans, roaming and device lines for a distributed team often sit outside the UC budget, and <a href="/tem/what-is-telecom-expense-management-tem/">telecom expense management</a> is how larger companies keep them visible. If you offer connectivity as an employee perk, see <a href="/telecom/connectivity-as-a-benefit/">connectivity as a benefit</a>.</p>
<figure><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" src="https://media.spenza.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/skype-meeting-tool-choice-orange.webp" alt="Three ribbons lead from a desk with folders, a browser window with calendar and mail, and speech bubbles, labelled Office suite, Workspace and Outside guests." width="1600" height="900" loading="lazy"><figcaption>The right meeting tool follows where the team already works.</figcaption></figure>
<h2 id="choose">Which should you choose</h2>
<p>Pick by the job in front of you rather than by a feature grid.</p><ul><li><strong>Microsoft 365 organization, internal collaboration:</strong> Teams. It is already in the subscription and keeps chat, files and meetings together.</li><li><strong>Google Workspace organization, quick browser calls:</strong> Google Meet. It launches from Calendar and Gmail.</li><li><strong>Meetings with outside clients and partners:</strong> Zoom, since it does not require either suite.</li><li><strong>Calls to ordinary phone numbers:</strong> a calling plan or separate calling service, priced separately.</li></ul><p>Running two tools is reasonable, for example Teams or Meet internally and Zoom for client-facing calls. That is not a failure to decide.</p>
<h2 id="conclusion">Conclusion</h2>
<p>Skype ended on May 5, 2025, and the export and number-porting windows closed in 2026, so there is nothing left to compare it against. Choose Teams for a Microsoft 365 company, Meet for Google Workspace, and Zoom for external meetings, then buy phone calling separately and test it with real numbers. The step most teams underestimate is the network. Measure bandwidth, move Teams traffic off the VPN, and give remote staff a path that does not depend only on home Wi-Fi.</p>
