<div id="spenza-merged-article" class="mh-article">
<p>In telecom, OSS means Operations Support Systems and BSS means Business Support Systems. If you were looking for open source software, that is a different OSS. This page covers the telecom meaning.</p>
<p>You have used both today. Choosing a phone plan, paying for it and getting a bill all ran through a BSS. Your SIM activating, your phone finding signal and an engineer fixing a faulty cell site before you noticed all ran through an OSS. One side takes the order. The other makes the network deliver it.</p>
<p>This guide also traces one eSIM order from checkout to first data session. Then it maps who owns each function when a brand launches an MVNO: a mobile virtual network operator, which sells service on another company&#8217;s network.</p>
<h2 id="what-are-oss-and-bss">What are OSS and BSS?</h2>
<p>OSS (Operations Support Systems) is the software a telecom operator uses to provision, monitor and maintain its network. BSS (Business Support Systems) is the software that manages customers, products, orders, charging and billing. BSS takes the order and bills for it; OSS makes the network deliver it. Together they run every mobile, broadband and IoT service.</p>
<p>They meet twice on every order: when the BSS sends it to the OSS for activation, and when usage flows back so the BSS can charge for it.</p>
<h3 id="what-does-oss-stand-for-in-telecom">What does OSS stand for in telecom?</h3>
<p>OSS stands for Operations Support System, usually used in the plural as Operations Support Systems. You will also see Operational Support System. In GSM architecture documents, OSS meant the Operation and Support Subsystem, the part of a GSM network that monitored and controlled the other elements. All three names point to the same job: running the network.</p>
<h3 id="what-does-bss-stand-for-in-telecom">What does BSS stand for in telecom?</h3>
<p>In telecom, BSS stands for Business Support System, the software for customers, products, orders and billing. The same letters mean Base Station Subsystem in GSM radio networks and Basic Service Set in Wi-Fi. Our guide to the <a href="/telecom/bss-full-form/">other meanings of BSS</a> explains those.</p>

<h3 id="is-billing-part-of-oss-or-bss">Is billing part of OSS or BSS?</h3>
<p>Billing is BSS. The confusion comes from FCAPS, the ITU-T model of network management functions, which includes &#8220;accounting&#8221;: measuring how much of the network each subscriber uses and producing usage records. That measurement happens on the network side. Rating those records against a price plan, applying discounts and producing an invoice is BSS work.</p>
<p>In 5G, 3GPP places real-time charging in a Charging Function (CHF) within its converged charging architecture, defined in <a href="https://www.3gpp.org/DynaReport/32240.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener">3GPP TS 32.240</a> and <a href="https://www.3gpp.org/DynaReport/32290.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener">TS 32.290</a>. The industry usually counts that charging system as BSS. It matters most if you plan to sell <a href="/telecom/telecom-5g-standalone-vs-non-standalone/">5G Standalone</a> services with usage-based pricing.</p>

<h2 id="what-an-oss-does">What an OSS does</h2>
<p>An OSS system (Operations Support System) is a collection of software tools that telecom providers use to manage, monitor and maintain their network infrastructure. Most operators run OSS products from several vendors, grouped around four jobs.</p>
<h3 id="service-fulfilment-provisioning-and-activation">Service fulfilment: provisioning and activation</h3>
<p>Fulfilment turns a sold product into a working service. The OSS works out what has to change in the network, sends the configuration to the right elements and confirms the result. For a mobile line that means the subscriber profile, the SIM or eSIM profile, and the data policy and allowance.</p>
<h3 id="network-and-service-inventory">Network and service inventory</h3>
<p>Inventory records what exists and how it connects: physical equipment, logical resources such as IP addresses, IMSI and ICCID ranges and phone numbers, and the <a href="https://www.matechco.com/customer-relationship-management/">CRM Services</a> help providers understand customer needs, personalize experiences, and reduce churn. When inventory is wrong, fulfilment allocates resources that are already in use, and assurance cannot tell which customers an alarm affects.</p>
<h3 id="service-assurance-faults-and-performance">Service assurance: faults and performance</h3>
<p>Assurance covers everything after activation. Fault management collects alarms, removes duplicates, links them to a probable cause and opens a trouble ticket. Performance management collects counters such as throughput, latency and dropped sessions and compares them with thresholds. For an IoT fleet, the same function shows up as per-SIM usage and connection status.</p>
<h3 id="network-and-element-management-nms-and-ems">Network and element management (NMS and EMS)</h3>
<p>An element management system (EMS) manages one vendor&#8217;s equipment. A network management system (NMS) sits above several EMSs and gives a single cross-vendor view. The split comes from the layered model in <a href="https://www.itu.int/rec/T-REC-M.3010/en" target="_blank" rel="noopener">ITU-T Recommendation M.3010</a>, the Telecommunications Management Network (TMN) standard.</p>
<h3 id="the-fcaps-model">The FCAPS model</h3>
<p>FCAPS names the five management functional areas set out in <a href="https://www.itu.int/rec/T-REC-M.3400/en" target="_blank" rel="noopener">ITU-T Recommendation M.3400</a>: fault, configuration, accounting, performance and security. OSS requests for proposal still use it to group requirements.</p>

<h2 id="what-is-changing-in-oss-and-bss-and-what-is-not">What is changing in OSS and BSS, and what is not</h2>
<p>Operators are replacing large suites with components that expose standard APIs. TM Forum&#8217;s <a href="https://www.tmforum.org/oda/implementation" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Open Digital Architecture</a> defines those components and their Open APIs. Its <a href="https://convergedigest.com/tm-forum-expands-open-digital-architecture-with-new-certification/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">ODA Component Conformance Certification</a> became generally available in June 2025, by which time nearly 100 companies had certified more than 1,700 Open API implementations. For a buyer, that makes &#8220;open&#8221; something you can check: ask which of a vendor&#8217;s APIs are certified.</p>
<p>Network operations are moving from alarms that wait for a person to closed loops that diagnose and fix problems on their own. TM Forum grades this on six levels, from Level 0 (manual) to Level 5 (fully autonomous). In its <a href="https://tmforum.org/news-insight/newsroom/autonomous-networks-leadership-forum-spotlights-level-4-shift" target="_blank" rel="noopener">survey of 80 operators</a>, 20 percent expected to reach Level 4 or above by 2027, and 81 percent were targeting it by 2030. Automation needs accurate inventory and real-time events, so a siloed OSS and BSS slows it down.</p>
<p>Smaller operators and brands also buy OSS and BSS as a service instead of licensing software, a model covered in our guide to <a href="/telecom/what-is-telecom-as-a-service-taas/">Telecom-as-a-Service (TaaS)</a>.</p>
<p>The split itself has stayed put. Someone still takes the order and someone still makes the network deliver it. Cloud-native stacks move that boundary into APIs, and the boundary still decides where orders stall.</p>

<div class="mh-faq"><details open="">
<h3 id="faq-1">What is the difference between OSS and BSS?</h3>
<div class="mh-faq-answer">
<p>OSS (Operations Support Systems) manages the network: provisioning, inventory, fault and performance management. BSS (Business Support Systems) manages the business: products, customers, orders, charging and billing. BSS takes the order and bills for it. OSS makes the network deliver the service and keeps it working.</p>
</div>
</details><details>
<h3 id="faq-2">What does OSS stand for in telecom?</h3>
<div class="mh-faq-answer">
<p>OSS stands for Operations Support System, also written Operational Support System. It is the software an operator uses to provision services, track network resources, detect faults and monitor performance. Older GSM documents use OSS for the Operation and Support Subsystem, the part of a GSM network that monitored and controlled the other elements.</p>
</div>
</details><details>
<h3 id="faq-3">Is billing part of OSS or BSS?</h3>
<div class="mh-faq-answer">
<p>Billing is part of BSS. The network side measures usage and produces usage records, which the FCAPS model calls accounting. The BSS rates those records against the customer&#8217;s plan, applies discounts and taxes, and issues the invoice. Real-time charging, including the 5G Charging Function, is usually counted as BSS.</p>
</div>
</details><details>
<h3 id="faq-4">How do OSS and BSS work together?</h3>
<div class="mh-faq-answer">
<p>They exchange data at two points. When a customer orders, the BSS validates the order and sends service and resource requests to the OSS, which activates the service and reports back. When the customer uses the service, the network reports usage to the BSS charging system, which rates it and bills for it.</p>
</div>
</details><details>
<h3 id="faq-5">Does an MVNO need its own OSS and BSS?</h3>
<div class="mh-faq-answer">
<p>An MVNO needs OSS and BSS functions, but most do not build them. A light MVNO uses an MVNE platform for provisioning, charging, billing and self-care, while the host operator runs the network. A full MVNO that operates its own core network usually runs more of the stack itself.</p>
</div>
</details><details>
<h3 id="faq-6">What are examples of OSS and BSS systems?</h3>
<div class="mh-faq-answer">
<p>OSS examples include network inventory, service activation, fault management, performance monitoring, and element or network management systems. BSS examples include the product catalogue, CRM, order management, online charging, billing and revenue assurance. Large suppliers to mobile operators include Amdocs, Ericsson, Netcracker, Nokia and Oracle. MVNOs usually get these functions from an MVNE instead.</p>
</div>
</details><details>
<h3 id="faq-7">What is FCAPS in telecom?</h3>
<div class="mh-faq-answer">
<p>FCAPS stands for fault, configuration, accounting, performance and security: the five management functional areas in ITU-T Recommendation M.3400. Operators and vendors use it to group network management requirements. In FCAPS, accounting means measuring usage. Turning that usage into an invoice is a BSS job.</p>
</div>
</details><details>
<h3 id="faq-8">What is replacing legacy OSS/BSS systems?</h3>
<div class="mh-faq-answer">
<p>Cloud-native components connected through standard APIs are replacing monolithic suites, guided by TM Forum&#8217;s Open Digital Architecture (ODA) and Open APIs. Many MVNOs skip the software purchase and use OSS and BSS functions as a managed MVNE platform.</p>
