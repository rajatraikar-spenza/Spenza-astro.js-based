A conference can help you compare approaches, meet an operator or find the right technical contact. It cannot show that a product works on your devices or that a commercial agreement covers your countries.</p>

<h2 id="choose-audience">How do you choose the right audience and meetings?</h2>
<p>Choose the event where you can meet the people who own your unresolved decisions. Start with the deployment or commercial problem, name the roles needed to address it, and only then look at the agenda. A long exhibitor list matters less than access to one relevant specialist.</p>
<p>For an IoT engineering team, the brief might cover the device model, modem firmware, target countries, installation environment and reporting pattern. Bring the conditions that make your fleet hard to serve, such as indoor placement, long sleep periods, intermittent coverage or an expensive field visit, and ask who can evaluate them after the event. If you are still choosing a radio technology, read the <a href="/esim/nb-iot-vs-lte-m-vs-5g-redcap/">NB-IoT, LTE-M and 5G RedCap comparison</a> before you build a vendor shortlist, and use the <a href="/iot/iot-connectivity-comparison/">IoT connectivity comparison</a> to structure the questions.</p>
<p>An MVNO team needs a different brief: the target customer, the service geography, who is responsible for launch tasks and which systems already exist. The <a href="/mvno/how-mvnes-accelerate-mvno-launch/">guide to how MVNEs accelerate an MVNO launch</a> shows which responsibilities can stay with the brand, which helps you decide whether you need a host operator, an enablement partner or a specialist supplier.</p>
<p>Write one decision for each planned meeting. “Understand the platform” is hard to act on. “Confirm who owns failed activation recovery for our proposed device family” gives the supplier something concrete. Share the brief beforehand and ask whether the attendee can answer it or will bring the person who can. Then tailor the questions to the role:</p>
<ul><li><strong>Product and engineering:</strong> supported device configurations, integration requirements and a route to technical validation.</li><li><strong>Operations:</strong> how exceptions are detected, assigned, resolved and recorded across suppliers.</li><li><strong>Finance and procurement:</strong> what the quote includes, which charges vary with behavior and which contract conditions remain open.</li><li><strong>Partnerships:</strong> who owns the next step and what each side must provide before a commercial decision.</li></ul>
<p>Keep technical vocabulary precise. An “eSIM ready” demonstration does not identify the provisioning architecture or show support for your fleet. Ask which specification applies, which device prerequisites exist and which workflow is supported. The <a href="/esim/sgp-22-vs-sgp-32-esim-standards-guide/">SGP.22 vs SGP.32 guide</a> explains why consumer and IoT provisioning raise different questions.</p>
<div class="mh-callout"><p><strong>Meeting check:</strong> Separate what is generally available from a roadmap item, a partner dependency or a demonstration setup. Record each answer with its date and scope so procurement can verify the same claim later.</p></div>
<h2 id="budget">What belongs in an event attendance budget?</h2>
<p>An event budget covers direct spending plus the team time to prepare, attend and follow up. Use real pass and travel quotations, not assumed industry averages, and weigh the full commitment against the decisions you expect to advance, including whether a remote meeting could answer the same questions.</p>
<div class="mh-table"><table><thead><tr><th>Budget item</th><th>Input to collect</th><th>Planning question</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Pass and meeting access</td><td>Current pass type, taxes and access conditions</td><td>Does it include the sessions or meeting facilities you need?</td></tr><tr><td>Travel and accommodation</td><td>Approved itinerary and booking terms</td><td>What changes if the meeting or travel plan moves?</td></tr><tr><td>Local costs</td><td>Transfers, meals and other permitted expenses</td><td>Which expenses belong in the same approval?</td></tr><tr><td>Preparation and attendance</td><td>Staff time and work coverage</td><td>Who writes the brief and covers operations while you are away?</td></tr><tr><td>Follow-up evaluation</td><td>Technical, procurement and partner time</td><td>Who can review evidence and run the next test?</td></tr></tbody></table></div>
<p>This worksheet supplies no prices or return assumptions. Fill it with approved quotations and your own time estimates. A free exhibition pass can still cost travel and working days, and it may not include paid conference sessions, so read the organizer&#8217;s access conditions before treating it as equivalent to another pass. Keep optional upgrades on a separate line so the team can see which part of the budget enables a specific meeting.</p>
<p>Set success criteria you can verify: a named technical owner, a documented support boundary, a complete quotation or an agreed evaluation plan. Meetings and contacts are activity measures. They do not, on their own, show revenue, savings or a deployment outcome.</p>
<p>Use the same discipline for connectivity purchasing afterward. A low headline rate is one input among several. The <a href="/telecom/iot-connectivity-cost-guide-2025/">IoT connectivity cost guide</a> covers the usage, lifecycle and operational assumptions you need for a comparable estimate, and you should ask every supplier to price the same defined workload rather than unrelated example plans.</p>
<div class="mh-callout"><p><strong>Budget check:</strong> Do not assign a standard return to attending an event. Record your own costs and the decisions that followed, then separate a result you can attribute from a conversation that might have happened anyway.</p></div>
<h2 id="procurement-evidence">How do event conversations become procurement evidence?</h2>
<p>They become evidence when each claim is recorded, confirmed by the responsible supplier and tested against an agreed scope. Keep the original question, the answer and its conditions together, and give every promising discussion an owner for the next step.</p>
<ul><li><strong>Capture the claim.</strong> Record the product, contact, date and conditions, and note whether the answer came from a live demonstration, a document or a verbal explanation.</li><li><strong>Request supporting material.</strong> Ask for product documentation, service terms or an integration example, and identify anything still dependent on a carrier, partner or future release.</li><li><strong>Agree an evaluation.</strong> Specify the device, market and workflow to test, including an exception case, and define what a pass or an unresolved result means.</li><li><strong>Close the decision.</strong> Record fit, remaining conditions and the accountable owner. A rejected or deferred option is a useful outcome when the reason is written down.</li></ul>
<p>A supplier demonstration shows behavior under the conditions chosen for that demonstration. It does not establish performance on your devices, networks or installation sites, so it cannot replace a pilot. For a connectivity management platform, a polished dashboard should lead to questions about the systems behind it: how delayed usage, partial activation failures and support escalation appear in operational records. The <a href="/esim/leading-iot-connectivity-management-platforms/">guide to leading IoT connectivity management platforms</a> separates SIM and connectivity control from device firmware management, so you do not judge one product against another product&#8217;s responsibilities. The <a href="/telecom/single-pane-of-glass-iot-connectivity-management/">single-pane-of-glass explainer</a> covers what a unified view of several carriers should and should not include.</p>
<p>Keep a common record across suppliers. Share the same fleet assumptions and evaluation criteria, then note where responses are comparable and where they still need clarification. A proposal that states its limits is more useful than a broad claim with no conditions.</p>

<p>Start with your operating problem, deployment scope and the responsibilities you want a partner to cover. Share the countries, the device or subscriber context, the systems you already run and the decisions still open. Published customer cases can frame the discussion, but their results should not be assumed to apply to your project.</p>
<p>The <a href="/spenza-product/butlr-iot-spenza-casestudy/">Butlr case study</a> describes an IoT regional rollout, which makes it a starting point for talking about expanding a sensor deployment across countries. The <a href="/spenza-product/rocketphone-case-study/">RocketPhone case study</a> covers an enterprise MVNO launch and suits questions about the service, platform responsibilities and implementation approach. Neither shows that a conference meeting caused the project, and neither guarantees the same outcome elsewhere.</p>

<h2 id="conclusion">Conclusion</h2>
<p>Pick an event around a decision you need to make, verify the current edition on the organizer&#8217;s own page and book the meetings before you book the flight. For the rest of 2026 that means MVNO Nation Europe or Smart City Expo World Congress on 3 to 5 November, or MVNOs World Connect on 1 December. For IoT connectivity, the 2027 anchors are CES, IoT Tech Expo Global, MWC Barcelona and embedded world. Bring a clear brief, budget for the work after the event and treat every supplier claim as something to confirm. A useful next step has a named owner, a defined scope and a record of what remains uncertain.</p>
