<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Best MVNO Plans &amp; Carriers for 2026 | Top Picks by Network</strong></h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">An <a href="/mvno/mvno/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">MVNO </a>(mobile virtual network operator) sells phone service on Verizon&#8217;s, T-Mobile&#8217;s or AT&amp;T&#8217;s towers under its own brand. One unlimited line costs $25 to $35 a month at our four network picks, against $50 to $110 at the big three. Coverage comes from the host network, so the best MVNO is the best brand on the network that works at your address.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Our picks for September 2026:</p>



<ol class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>On Verizon</strong>: Visible, $25 a month with taxes and fees included.</li>



<li><strong>On T-Mobile</strong>: Mint Mobile, $30 a month for unlimited data on a 12 month plan, and $15 a month for a new customer&#8217;s first term.</li>



<li><strong>On AT&amp;T</strong>: Cricket Wireless, $35 a month with taxes included, or four lines for $100.</li>



<li><strong>On any of the three</strong>: US Mobile, $25 a month with taxes included, and you choose the network.</li>
</ol>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The trade off is data priority. On most low cost plans your data waits behind the host carrier&#8217;s own customers when a tower is busy. Spenza is an MVNE: we activate lines on all three US networks and run billing for MVNOs. This guide uses that inside view to show who owns each brand, how priority works, and what each plan costs after taxes.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Why an MVNO can charge less for the same network</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Spenza sits on the supply side of this market, so we see where the money goes. An MVNO buys network access from the host carrier at a wholesale rate. It builds no towers and bids on no spectrum. Most MVNOs sell online, run support through an app and take payment up front, which removes store rent, credit checks and unpaid bills. Many also accept a lower priority class for their traffic and stream video in standard definition, and both choices shrink their network bill.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Your phone still connects to the same towers. The savings come from the business model around the network.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Cheaper has not meant less satisfied</strong></h3>

Power&#8217;s 2026 Wireless Carrier Satisfaction Study, Volume 2</a> surveyed 57,339 customers on a 1,000 point scale. Consumer Cellular scored 720 and Mint Mobile 711. T-Mobile, the highest ranked network operator, scored 636 among postpaid customers.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>MVNO vs Major Carriers: Pros, Cons &amp; the One Trade-Off That Matters</strong></h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">An MVNO can give you access to a major carrier&#8217;s network for roughly $25 to $45 a month, often at a fraction of the $50 to $110 you might pay for a direct carrier plan. The trade-off is usually lower data priority when a network is congested, app-based or online support, and fewer phone deals or financing options.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">For light to moderate users, the math can make sense. If you use less than 30 GB a month and your usual towers are rarely congested, an MVNO can offer most of the network experience at a lower monthly cost. The catch is that the difference becomes much more noticeable when you&#8217;re using your phone in crowded places at busy times.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The major carriers also deprioritize some of their own lower-cost plans. T-Mobile, for example, says its Essentials plan may be subject to lower network priority during congestion, while Verizon&#8217;s network management policy applies to certain plans. In other words, even a direct carrier customer is not necessarily at the top of the queue.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Deprioritization and throttling are two different slowdowns</strong></h3>



<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter size-large is-resized"><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" width="1024" height="512" src="https://media.spenza.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/image-3-1024x512.png" alt="Deprioritization and throttling are often lumped together, but they work differently.
" class="wp-image-22545" style="width:666px;height:auto" srcset="https://media.spenza.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/image-3-1024x512.png 1024w, https://media.spenza.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/image-3-300x150.png 300w, https://media.spenza.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/image-3-768x384.png 768w, https://media.spenza.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/image-3-1536x768.png 1536w, https://media.spenza.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/image-3.png 1774w" sizes="(max-width: 1024px) 100vw, 1024px" /></figure>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Deprioritization and throttling are often lumped together, but they work differently.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Every data session on an LTE or 5G network is assigned a quality-of-service priority. The 3GPP standards define QCI for LTE and 5QI for 5G. While a cell has plenty of spare capacity, that priority may make little practical difference. When the cell becomes congested, however, higher-priority traffic gets served first.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">That&#8217;s deprioritization. A carrier assigns different priority levels to different plans or brands, and a lower-priority connection may slow down when the tower is busy. The important part is that the slowdown is conditional. Once congestion clears, speeds can return to normal.<a href="https://www.verizon.com/about/our-company/network-management" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener"> Verizon&#8217;s network policy</a> describes this directly: &#8220;<strong>Once the demand on the site lessens, or if you connect to a different site not experiencing high demand, your speed will return to normal.</strong>&#8220;</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Throttling is different. It is a fixed speed reduction that kicks in after a defined threshold, regardless of whether the tower is congested. Google Fi, for example, reduces speeds to 256 kbps after the applicable high-speed data threshold on certain plans. Boost Mobile has plans that reduce speeds to 512 kbps after 30 GB, while some Mint plans reduce speeds to 2G levels after the included high-speed allotment is used.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The exact treatment varies by brand and plan, so the fine print matters.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">In practice, deprioritization is most likely to show up in places such as stadiums, airports, crowded commuter trains and dense downtown areas during peak hours. On a quiet suburban tower, you may never notice it.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">That&#8217;s why the most useful test is not a speed test from your living room. Use the carrier&#8217;s free trial, where available, and test the service at the places and times that matter to you.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>How to Choose an MVNO in 2026: 4 Key Questions</strong></h2>



<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter size-large is-resized"><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" width="819" height="1024" src="https://media.spenza.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/image-4-819x1024.png" alt="How to Choose an MVNO in 2026: 4 Key Questions" class="wp-image-22547" style="width:555px;height:auto" srcset="https://media.spenza.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/image-4-819x1024.png 819w, https://media.spenza.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/image-4-240x300.png 240w, https://media.spenza.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/image-4-768x960.png 768w, https://media.spenza.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/image-4.png 1200w" sizes="(max-width: 819px) 100vw, 819px" /></figure>



<ol class="wp-block-list">
<li>Which network works where you live and work? Check your home and work addresses on the <a href="https://www.verizon.com/coverage-map/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">Verizon</a>, <a href="https://www.t-mobile.com/coverage/coverage-map" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">T-Mobile</a> and <a href="https://www.att.com/maps/wireless-coverage.html" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">AT&amp;T</a> maps, then cross check on the <a href="https://broadbandmap.fcc.gov/home" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">FCC&#8217;s National Broadband Map</a>. Maps predict outdoor signal. Your kitchen is the real test, and a free trial settles it.</li>



<li>How much data do you use? Open your current carrier&#8217;s app and read the last three bills. Under 10 GB, buy a capped plan. Between 10 and 50 GB, a $25 unlimited plan fits. Above 50 GB, or with a laptop on the hotspot, buy a premium tier.</li>



<li>Are your usual towers crowded? If your data crawls at the stadium, on the train or downtown at 6 pm, pay for priority: Visible+ at $35, US Mobile Unlimited Premium at $44, Total Wireless MAX 5G, or Google Fi.</li>



<li>What will 12 months cost? Add taxes, the AutoPay condition, the renewal price and any upfront payment, then compare totals.</li>
</ol>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">A worked example for question 4. Mint unlimited on a 3 month intro, then renewed for 12 months, costs $45 plus nine months at $30: $315 before taxes for the first year. Visible costs $300 for the same year on its monthly plan with taxes included, $275 on its annual plan, or $228 with the SAVE6 code. Mint&#8217;s 12 month intro costs $180 before taxes, and wins year one by a wide margin. The cheapest brand depends on the term you choose.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>How to Switch to an MVNO &amp; Keep Your Number in 2026</strong></h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Test first, port second, and do not cancel your old line yourself. The port closes it for you.</p>



<ol class="wp-block-list">
<li>Confirm the phone is unlocked and supports eSIM. Our <a href="/esim-compatibility-checker/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">eSIM compatibility checker</a> covers the second part, and the unlock rules are in the table below.</li>



<li>Test the network for a week at the places and hours you use the phone. <li>Collect your account number and a transfer PIN from your current carrier, and switch off any number lock or port out protection.</li>

<li>Start the port from the new carrier&#8217;s app. FCC rules require simple ports to finish in one business day, and the <a href="https://www.fcc.gov/general/wireless-local-number-portability-wlnp" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">FCC&#8217;s wireless porting page</a> puts wireless to wireless transfers at about two and a half hours.</li>



<li>Expect no resistance. The FCC says your old company &#8220;cannot refuse to port your number, even if you owe money&#8221;. You still owe any balance and any phone installments.</li>



<li>After the port, set up voicemail, check iMessage or RCS, and test the text codes from your bank.</li>
</ol>
