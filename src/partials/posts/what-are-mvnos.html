What Is a Mobile Virtual Network Operator (MVNO)

A Mobile Virtual Network Operator (MVNO) is a wireless provider that sells mobile services without owning cell towers or spectrum licenses. Instead, an MVNO leases network capacity in bulk from a Mobile Network Operator (MNO) like AT&T, T-Mobile, or Verizon, then resells that access under its own brand with its own pricing.

The simplest way to think about it: when you make a call or use data on an MVNO plan, your signal travels through the same towers as a direct carrier customer. The coverage is identical. The difference is that the MVNO handles your billing, customer support, and plan design, and typically charges 30 to 50 percent less because it has no network infrastructure to maintain.

Here is how the process works in practice. The MVNO signs a wholesale agreement with a host MNO to buy network capacity at bulk rates. It then designs its own data plans, added services, and customer experience. Subscribers sign up through the MVNO’s website or app, activate a SIM card or eSIM, and their service runs on the host MNO’s network infrastructure. The MVNO earns revenue on the difference between wholesale cost and retail price. Some MVNOs work directly with MNOs. Others partner with a Mobile Virtual Network Enabler (MVNE) like Spenza, which provides the billing systems, SIM management, provisioning, and carrier integrations so the MVNO can launch faster and avoid building a technology stack from scratch.

Types of Virtual Network Operator (MVNO) Models

Not every MVNO operates the same way. The level of control, investment, and infrastructure involvement varies by model. Here is a quick comparison:

MVNO Type Control Level Infrastructure Owned Investment Best For
Branded Reseller (Skinny MVNO) Low None (uses host MNO for everything) Low ($10K–$100K) Fast market entry, brand-focused companies
Thin MVNO Low–Medium Billing and CRM only Medium ($100K–$300K) Startups wanting basic customization
Light MVNO Medium Billing, SIM management, customer support Medium ($200K–$1M) Growing brands needing more control
Thick MVNO Medium–High Core switching, HLR/HSS, billing High ($500K–$3M) Enterprise and IoT-focused operators
Full MVNO High Complete core network (minus RAN) Very High ($1M–$10M+) Maximum independence and margin control

A Light MVNO is the most common choice for startups. You get enough control over customer care and billing to differentiate your brand, without the heavy cost of core network ownership. A Full MVNO makes sense for large-scale IoT deployments where you need direct control over SIM card provisioning and data routing.

Advantages and Disadvantages of MVNOs

MVNOs offer several compelling advantages over traditional mobile network operators (MNOs). Because they do not bear the enormous costs of building and maintaining network infrastructure, such as cell towers, spectrum licenses, and fiber backhaul, MVNOs can focus their resources on delivering a superior customer experience at lower prices. However, they also have a few disadvantages, including data deprioritization and roaming restrictions.

Advantages Disadvantages
Cost effective plans: save 30 to 50% vs. major carriers. The average U.S. family overpaid by roughly $2,200/year in 2025. Data deprioritization: during peak congestion, data speeds may slow. Premium MVNO tiers eliminate this.
No-contract flexibility: switch, cancel, or upgrade any time without fees. Fewer perks: no bundled streaming or device trade-in programs, though savings usually offset this.
Same network coverage: MVNOs use the exact same towers as AT&T, Verizon, and T-Mobile. Roaming limits: some MVNOs cannot roam on partner networks the way direct carrier customers can.
eSIM support: activate a new plan in minutes from your phone, no store visit needed. Customer support variability: smaller MVNOs may have limited support hours. Stick with established names.
Specialized data plans: tailored for IoT devices, seniors, students, international travelers, and businesses. Limited 5G access: some budget tiers restrict 5G. Premium plans typically include full 5G.

How to Choose the Right MVNO for You

With dozens of MVNO carriers available, selecting the right one requires a structured approach. Use this framework to evaluate your options:

1. Check coverage first. Go to the carrier coverage map for your area. If Verizon has the best signal where you live and work, focus on MVNOs on Verizon’s network like Visible or US Mobile Warp.

2. Match data usage to a plan tier. Under 5GB per month? Tello at $8/mo or US Mobile at $10/mo will handle it. Heavy user? Visible at $25/mo gives you unlimited data with a mobile hotspot.

3. Confirm your phone works. Make sure your device is unlocked and compatible with the MVNO’s host network. Most modern phones support all U.S. bands. Check eSIM support if you want instant activation.

4. Read the fine print on data speeds. “Unlimited” does not always mean full speed. Many MVNOs throttle after a threshold. Premium tiers from Visible+ and US Mobile remove deprioritization for heavy users.

MVNOs for Business: IoT Deployments, Enterprise Mobility, and New Revenue Streams

The MVNO model extends far beyond consumer phone plans. In 2026, businesses across retail, fintech, healthcare, logistics, and manufacturing are leveraging mobile virtual network operators to power IoT connected devices, manage enterprise mobility, and even create entirely new revenue streams by offering branded mobile services to their own customers.

IoT and MVNO: A Perfect Match

IoT deployments are one of the fastest-growing segments in the MVNO market, with IoT MVNO lines increasing at a 16.95% CAGR in the U.S. alone. IoT devices, including smart meters, fleet trackers, connected medical equipment, and retail point-of-sale systems, require reliable, cost-effective cellular connectivity that traditional carrier plans are often too expensive or inflexible to provide.

MVNOs solve this by offering specialized data plans designed for IoT use cases: low-bandwidth plans for sensors, pooled data for device fleets, and global SIM cards or eSIMs that work across multiple countries without manual configuration. A Full MVNO or Thick MVNO model gives IoT operators direct control over SIM card provisioning, real-time usage monitoring, and network routing, critical capabilities for large-scale IoT deployments.

FAQs

What does MVNO stand for?

MVNO stands for Mobile Virtual Network Operator, a mobile service provider that does not own its own network and resells access bought from a major carrier.

How does an MVNO work?

MVNOs lease network capacity from larger carriers at wholesale rates and then sell voice, text, and data to customers under their own brand and pricing.

Can I keep my phone number when switching to an MVNO?

Yes. Most MVNOs allow number porting so you can transfer your existing number when you activate the new plan.

What MVNO uses Verizon’s network?

Several MVNOs use Verizon’s network, including Visible, US Mobile (Warp plan), Xfinity Mobile, Total Wireless, Twigby, Straight Talk, Red Pocket, and Page Plus. Visible is widely considered the best Verizon MVNO for unlimited plans, while US Mobile offers the most flexibility by also supporting AT&T and T-Mobile.

Is US Mobile a good MVNO?

Yes. US Mobile is one of the most highly rated MVNO carriers in 2026. It offers access to all three major networks (Verizon, AT&T, T-Mobile), flexible pricing starting at $10/month.

Is MVNO coverage as good as a major carrier?

Yes. MVNOs use the same underlying network infrastructure as their host carriers, so coverage quality is generally similar.

Are there downsides to using an MVNO?

Some MVNOs may deprioritize data speeds during peak times, and support or perks can vary by provider.

What is a Mobile Virtual Network Enabler (MVNE)?

An MVNE is a behind-the-scenes platform that provides the technology infrastructure MVNOs need to operate, including billing systems, SIM management, provisioning, customer support tools, and carrier integrations.
