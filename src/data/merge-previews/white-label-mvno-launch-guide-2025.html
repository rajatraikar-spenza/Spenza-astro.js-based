Think of it like opening a coffee shop inside a grocery store. You did not build the store and you do not manage the supply chain, but every cup that goes out has your name on it. Or in software terms: you launch a fintech product on Stripe instead of building a banking system.

That is a white label MVNO. You sell a branded mobile service running on an existing carrier network, with an MVNE platform handling billing, provisioning and operations underneath. Launch timelines run from 7 days to 90 days depending on the model. According to a GSMA study the white label MVNO market was worth $313 million in 2024 and is projected to cross $1 billion by 2029, and the window to get in early is still open.

This guide covers what the model actually includes, where white label ends and private label begins, what it costs, the launch sequence, the compliance you cannot skip, and how to choose the platform underneath it.

What Is a White Label MVNO?

A white label MVNO is a branded mobile service built on someone else's infrastructure. You own the brand, the pricing, the customer relationship and the margin. Your MVNE partner owns the carrier agreements, the network integration and the systems that keep it running.

A complete white label package — what vendors often market as "MVNO in a box" — includes:

  • White-label branding: customer-facing apps, portals and activation flows carrying your identity rather than the platform's.
  • Carrier connectivity: wholesale access to one or more host networks, contracted by the MVNE rather than by you.
  • BSS/OSS: billing, rating, plan lifecycle, SIM and eSIM provisioning, subscriber management.
  • API integrations: developer-facing APIs that connect to your existing systems, e-commerce platform and customer apps.
  • Customer support infrastructure: tooling for inquiries, connectivity troubleshooting and ticket routing.
  • Compliance tooling: the registrations and safeguards a US mobile service is required to carry.

The boundary worth being precise about: a white label MVNO is not a reseller passing through someone else's plans under someone else's brand. You set the plans, you set the prices, you own the subscriber. What you do not own is the network or the stack.

You Do Not Need to Be a Telco

This is the part most brands get stuck on. Mobile service feels like a telecom business, so it feels like it needs telecom people. It does not any more, for three reasons that all landed at once.

  • eSIM removed the logistics. Over a billion smartphone connections are expected to move to eSIM, which takes physical SIM inventory, packaging and shipping out of the launch entirely.
  • API-first platforms made the backend plug-and-play. What used to be months of custom telecom integration is now configuration.
  • The carriers are commoditising. Brands that build loyalty-driven mobile services — a retail chain offering data plans to its loyalty members — are capturing margin the carriers used to own.

The result is that fintechs, neobanks, retailers, IoT brands, MSPs, creator brands and even celebrities are launching white label MVNOs at a pace the industry has not seen before. A smartwatch sold without connectivity feels incomplete. A travel company offering roaming plans adds obvious value. That is where this model fits.

Quick fact. The US MVNO market was worth roughly $30 billion in 2024 and is projected to reach $52.9 billion by 2032, according to Allied Market Research. Many MVNOs operate at 15–40% profit margins depending on pricing and scale, and those margins improve when connectivity is bundled with a product the customer already buys.

White label is Shopify. Private label is building your own e-commerce platform. If you are starting out, white label is the entry point — the fastest and cheapest way to test whether a branded mobile service resonates before committing to a full build. Many businesses start white label and transition to private label as they scale, which is a deliberate path rather than a failure of the first choice.

Three things to factor in beyond the table:

  • MNO wholesale commitments. Most host carriers want minimum volume commitments. Negotiate carefully — this is where margin is won or lost.
  • SIM and eSIM provisioning. Charged per line and varies by carrier.
  • Support and billing. If the platform does not include them, budget separately. See how MVNO billing platforms compare before deciding.
The margin caveat. 15–40% is achievable, but only if you negotiate wholesale rates properly and pick a platform with pricing flexibility. Rigid plan structures compress margins quickly, and competing purely on price compresses them permanently. Profitable white label MVNOs raise lifetime value through bundling rather than cutting headline price.

How to Launch a White Label MVNO

Ten steps, in the order they actually happen:

  1. Identify your niche. Who is the subscriber, and what problem does the plan solve that a carrier plan does not?
  2. Do the market research. Competitor pricing, real demand, and how your customers actually use data.
  3. Choose your model. White label for speed and lower cost, private label for control. See the full breakdown of MVNO types.
  4. Pick your MVNE platform. The most consequential decision on this list. More below.
  5. Secure host carrier access. Your MVNE usually handles this, but understand which networks you will run on and on what terms.
  6. Configure the platform. Admin portal, APIs, dashboards, billing rules, SIM provisioning logic.
  7. Design plans and pricing. Build for your margin target and your brand, not for the bottom of the market.
  8. Brand everything. SIM packaging, eSIM QR codes, customer portal, activation flow, app.
  9. Handle the regulatory requirements. FCC registration, E911, CALEA, CPNI. Start early; this is the step that slips.
  10. Launch and market. Start small, instrument everything, iterate, then scale.

What a 7-Day Launch Actually Looks Like

Most providers quote months. For branded reseller and light white label models on a modern MVNE, the sequence compresses to a week:

Day 1Platform setup — account, API access, admin portal6
Days 2–3Plan design and branding — pricing, bundles, brand experience, margins7, 8
Days 4–5SIM and eSIM setup — connectivity, SIM profile, eSIM QR flow6
Days 6–7Testing, activation and go-live — end-to-end QA, test activations10

The honest qualifier: seven days applies to the branded reseller and light white label models, and assumes steps 1 to 5 are already settled before the clock starts. Full white label builds with custom billing and deeper integrations typically run three to six weeks. Both are well under the industry average, and neither is a guarantee — scope changes are what move these dates.

The differences come down to speed, flexibility and control. IPLOOK and Plintron are infrastructure-heavy and better suited to telecom operators; Gigs is oriented to fintech. Whichever you shortlist, ask the same five questions: can you run on more than one carrier, does the billing support your pricing model, is eSIM native or bolted on, who is carrier of record for compliance, and what happens to your subscribers and numbers if you leave.

US Regulatory Essentials

  • FCC registration — required to operate as an MVNO in the US market.
  • CALEA compliance — your platform must support lawful intercept under the Communications Assistance for Law Enforcement Act.
  • E911 — subscribers must be able to reach emergency services. Non-negotiable.
  • CPNI — Customer Proprietary Network Information rules govern how you handle and share subscriber data.

Compliance delay is one of the most common reasons a launch slips past its planned date. Most modern MVNE platforms streamline it, and an MVNE acting as carrier of record absorbs much of it, but you remain responsible for understanding what applies to your service. Frequently Asked Questions

What is a white label MVNO?

A branded mobile service you sell on an existing carrier network, with an MVNE platform handling billing, provisioning and operations. You own the brand, the pricing and the subscriber; you do not own the network or the stack.

What is the difference between white label and private label?

Speed and control. White label launches in 7–30 days on a pre-built platform with moderate customisation. Private label takes 3–6 months, costs more, and gives you your own billing, CRM and support systems. Most brands start white label and move to private label as volume justifies it.

How much does a white label MVNO cost to launch?

$10K–$100K setup for a branded reseller, $100K–$400K for a full white label build, and $2M+ for a private label stack. Monthly platform fees run from $500 to $20K depending on the model.

Can I really launch in 7 days?

For a branded reseller or light white label model, yes — provided your niche, model, carrier and platform decisions are already made when the week starts. Full white label builds with custom billing typically take three to six weeks.

Do I need to be a telecom company?

No. eSIM removed the SIM logistics, API-first platforms removed the integration work, and an MVNE can act as carrier of record for most compliance. What you need is a customer base and a reason for them to want a plan from you.

What margins can I expect?

15–40% depending on model, pricing and scale, and higher when connectivity is bundled with something the customer already buys. Margins depend far more on wholesale terms and plan design than on the platform you pick.

What happens if I want to change MVNE later?

It depends entirely on terms you agree before you sign. Confirm during selection who owns the subscriber data, the phone numbers and the SIM profiles if you leave. This is the single most commonly skipped question in MVNE evaluation.
