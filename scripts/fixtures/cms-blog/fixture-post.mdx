A connectivity bundle protects margin when the recurring price covers the data each device actually uses. Most losses come from the heaviest ten percent of devices.

The fix is arithmetic, not marketing: measure usage, set tiers above cost, and bill automatically.

## Why bundles lose money

Flat pricing hides the spread between light and heavy devices. The table below shows an illustrative spread.

| Tier | Monthly data | Illustrative cost | Margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Light | 100 MB | $0.40 | High |
| Typical | 1 GB | $2.50 | Medium |
| Heavy | 10 GB | $18.00 | Negative |

Illustrative numbers only; use your own wholesale rates.

## Setting tiers that hold

Start from cost, add the margin floor, then round to a price that reads cleanly.

### Use real usage data

Pull 90 days of usage, not a forecast.

## Automating the invoice

Usage records feed billing directly, so the invoice always matches the meter.

| Step | Owner | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Export usage | Platform | Usage file |
| Rate usage | Billing | Priced lines |
| Invoice | Finance | Customer invoice |

## Reviewing monthly

A short monthly review catches tier drift before it costs margin. See [Spenza contact](/contact-us/) for a walkthrough.

## Common mistakes

Do not set one price for every device. Do not forecast usage without data.

## Closing thoughts

Price the data, protect the margin, automate the bill.
