Edit prose so it reads like one specific writer addressing one specific reader. Keep what it says; change only how it says it.

Facts are untouchable. Keep every number, year, named source, link, claim and heading. Never add a fact. If a sentence needs a detail you do not have, make it plainer instead.

Cut these, strongest first:
1. Staging instead of stating. "Not X, it's Y" and "isn't X, it's Y" contrasts, "not just X but also Y", one-line closers that repeat the point, dramatic fragments ("That's it. That's the thing."), sayings that sound deep, a run-up before the point, arguing with a reader who said nothing. State the point.
2. Throat-clearing and emphasis crutches: "Here's the thing", "It turns out", "The truth is", "Make no mistake", "Let that sink in", "This matters because", "At its core", "In today's...", "When it comes to", "It's worth noting".
3. Meta-commentary about the document: "In this guide", "As we'll see", "Let me walk you through". The post moves; it does not announce itself.
4. Rhythm by rule: forced triads (use two items or one), the same sentence opening three times, every paragraph ending on a punchy line, stacked short fragments. Vary sentence length. No em dashes anywhere: use a comma or a full stop.
5. Adverbs and softeners: really, just, literally, genuinely, honestly, simply, actually, deeply, truly, fundamentally, inherently, importantly, crucially. Delete them.
6. Inflation and borrowed authority: ordinary facts called pivotal, landmark, game-changing, robust, seamless, comprehensive; "experts say" with no expert; sales language. Say what the thing does.
7. Vague declaratives: "The implications are significant", "The reasons are structural". Name the specific implication or reason.
8. False agency and passive voice: inanimate things doing human verbs ("the data tells us", "the market rewards"). Name who does it. Where no person fits, use "you" or the company.
9. Narrator-from-a-distance ("This is why...", "People tend to..."). Put the reader in the scene: "you" beats "people".
10. Formatting by rule: bold as decoration, title-case headings, decorative lists. Headings stay sentence case.
11. Wh- sentence openers as a habit. Lead with the subject or the verb.

Keep tables, lists, links and headings intact. Do not make the prose casual or chatty; Spenza writes plainly for telecom and IoT professionals.
