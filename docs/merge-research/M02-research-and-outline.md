### Intent verdict

The observed broad-query set favors definition-led explainers with recognizable examples, a mechanism, model distinctions, trade-offs and FAQs. Much of the visible competition serves consumer switching. M02 should satisfy that basic definition need immediately, then serve OEM, MSP and software-platform decisions with an operating-model comparison, scoped economics, launch checks and a published enterprise case. Do not turn the pillar into a consumer plan ranking or another 70-link directory.

What it takes to compete for the first result, without promising that outcome:

- Preserve the definition, role explanation, examples and launch sequence from the citation-bearing URLs, while correcting the radio/core distinction and universal claims.
- Make the business decision usable: responsibilities, cost inputs, acceptance evidence and six next-step spokes in under 3,000 words.
- Use RocketPhone's approved implementation to demonstrate the product requirement, delivered scope and partner responsibilities; clearly separate those facts from future expansion and unknown metrics.

### Proposed information-gain elements and evidence boundaries

1. **Product requirement to operating model, using RocketPhone.** Public case explains the mobile-call capture gap and the alternatives considered. M02 will connect that requirement to the need for the specified voice-path integration, instead of claiming every software platform needs a full MVNO. Evidence: case introduction, business problem and challenge. Scope it as RocketPhone's design rationale, not a universal statement that every handset workaround is impossible.
2. **Delivered scope versus future scope in a real enterprise launch.** Map the case's delivered provisioning, native voice/SMS/data, billing, enterprise accounts and reseller controls to the buyer's operating responsibilities. Clearly label future-market, self-service and downstream API proposals as future scope, not completed outcomes. Evidence: Solution, Implementation, Results and What Scaling Looks Like Next. The live Solution list currently has ten bullets; use capabilities rather than forcing a nine-item count.
3. **Product workflow plus existing channel distribution.** Explain the distinct value of SIP routing into the conversation platform and multi-tier reseller/enterprise administration. Evidence: mechanism paragraph, delivered SIP/API scope and multi-tier result. This is a concrete integration/distribution lesson, not a fabricated revenue or retention result.
4. **A reusable responsibility-and-proof worksheet.** For each function, ask who performs it, who owns the customer obligation, what is observable in a pilot and what is exportable on exit. This is an original editorial tool grounded in the source launch/risk sections and case scope. It is not represented as a tested Spenza benchmark or proprietary customer measurement.

No equivalent RocketPhone-specific treatment was found in the ten-page sample. This is a scoped observation, not proof that no top-ten result anywhere contains it. General ownership/economics frameworks already exist in long-tail competitors. The final information-gain score must assess the implemented case-based detail and distinct utility, not merely count tables or illustrations. No final QA score is awarded before the article exists.

### Semantic map

Core entity: mobile virtual network operator. Key relationships: MVNO purchases host network access; MNO supplies radio access; MVNE supplies agreed technical/operating services; MVNA aggregates wholesale access; providers can combine roles. Full-core control is distinct from owning the host radio access network.

Related entities: subscriber, device OEM, MSP, software platform, host MNO, MVNE, MVNA, radio access network, core network, SIM/eSIM, provisioning, business support system, rating, billing, customer care, wholesale agreement, service-level agreement, number portability, roaming, data priority, CRM, Session Initiation Protocol (SIP), Salesforce and RocketPhone.

Decision attributes: service scope, countries, host access, device compatibility, pricing authority, data rights, billing ownership, incident responsibility, wholesale minimums, support capacity and exit constraints. Economic attributes: revenue per active line, variable service costs, fixed operating costs, acquisition cost, retention and cash timing. Do not introduce unsupported target values.

### Table and callout specifications

Table 1: Visible/Verizon, Mint/T-Mobile and US Mobile's named network choices; no plan prices, superlatives or ownership classification beyond the official source. Source line dated September 2026 links official operator pages. Distinguish host-owned retail brands from independent wholesale arrangements where supported; do not call every example an independent company.

Table 2: compare responsibilities, not guaranteed margin or launch speed. Source line: CRTC's general infrastructure distinction plus the captured source sections, with a note that labels and contracts vary. Canadian registration rules are not applied worldwide.

Table 3: units include currency per active line per month, currency per activation, currency per GB and fixed currency per month; no numeric amounts without evidence. Formula is a planning identity, not a reported result: per-line contribution equals service revenue less attributable variable costs; positive contribution must also cover fixed costs. Minimum commitments and uneven usage can make simple linear break-even misleading.

Table 4: task / performing party to confirm / evidence to request. Include activation, lifecycle-to-invoice, support/escalation, customer-data export and service obligations. Entries are buyer checks, not undocumented claims about RocketPhone tests.
